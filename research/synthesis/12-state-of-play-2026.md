# 12 — State of Play: August 2026

*Project Lux · The current snapshot, in detail*

**Snapshot date: 25 August 2026.** Everything below is current as of this date and will decay.

---

## 1. The headline position

**Fusion has crossed from science into engineering, and the engineering has not yet been done.**

- Scientific breakeven: **achieved**, eleven times, at one facility, by one method ([[inertial-confinement|inertial confinement]])
- Net energy from a magnetically confined plasma: **not yet achieved**; attempt scheduled for **2027**
- Electricity delivered to a grid from fusion: **never**; earliest credible attempt **2028**
- Tritium self-sufficiency: **never demonstrated at any scale**
- A material qualified for a power plant's neutron environment: **no such material exists, and no facility can currently qualify one**

## 6. What changed structurally this year

Beyond individual results, five structural shifts occurred in 2026 that will outlast any single milestone.

### 6.1 A supply chain appeared

For the first time, fusion companies are selling to each other rather than each building everything:

- **CFS → Realta**: HTS magnets for Anvil and Hammir (April 2026)
- **Tokamak Energy → STEP**: magnet systems partner, £70 m
- **ORNL → Type One**: four cryogenic fuelling technologies (August 2026)
- **Kyoto Fusioneering → everyone**: gyrotrons, tritium systems, blanket technology
- **TAE ← Black Moon Energy**: helium-3 supply (August 2026)
- **ARPA-E → Thea**: $20 m for the first US modular HTS magnet line

This is what the transition from 56 prototypes to an industry looks like, and it happened this year.

### 6.2 Fusion entered the public markets

**General Fusion trades as GFUZ.** The FIA notes a second company in process. Quarterly reporting and a live ticker impose a discipline the sector has never faced, and the FIA's own framing is that this brings *"a different type of scrutiny."*

### 6.3 Regulation was settled, in the developers' favour

The NRC's **byproduct-material framework** (proposed 26 February 2026, final rule targeted October 2026) means most US fusion facilities will be licensed by **Agreement States**, not the NRC. Combined with the UK's statutory exclusion of fusion from nuclear site licensing, the two leading Western jurisdictions have removed regulatory uncertainty as a financing risk.

### 6.4 Governments rewrote themselves as industrial policy

DOE's roadmap (Parts I and II), the UK's March 2026 strategy and £2.5 bn, Germany's €2 bn Action Plan and three hubs, Japan's acceleration from ~2050 to the 2030s, Korea naming fusion a strategic technology, and China's 15th Five-Year Plan promising "extraordinary measures" — all within eighteen months, and all framed around commercialisation rather than research.

### 6.5 AI became the demand story

The FIA's foreword names it directly. Hyperscalers have committed **over 9.8 GW across 13 nuclear projects**; AI data-centre demand is projected to nearly triple to ~1,300 TWh by 2035. Fusion's two flagship offtake agreements are both with hyperscalers.

## 7. What is still completely missing

It is worth stating the gaps as plainly as the achievements.

| Missing capability | Nearest thing that exists | Earliest plausible |
|---|---|---|
| **Q > 1 in a magnetic device** | JET's Q = 0.67 (1997) | **SPARC, 2027** |
| **A burning plasma** (alpha-dominated) | NIF's hot spot, for ~100 ps | SPARC 2027 / ITER 2039 |
| **Tritium breeding demonstrated** | Small-scale experiments; ITER TBM from 2039 | UNITY-3 / [[librti|LIBRTI]], late 2020s |
| **A closed tritium fuel cycle** | Nothing | 2030s |
| **Fusion-spectrum materials qualification** | Surrogates; commercial DT neutron services | IFMIF-DONES / FPNS, 2030s |
| **Integrated [[divertor]] + burning core** | [[detachment|Detachment]] separately; JET's QCE/XPR in D-T | ITER, 2039 |
| **Remote replacement of activated in-vessel components** | ITER planning; no operational experience | 2030s |
| **Q_eng > 1** | Nothing, anywhere | 2030s at the very earliest |
| **Fusion electricity on a grid** | Nothing | Helion Orion, 2028 (aggressive) |
| **A fusion plant operating for a year at power** | Nothing | 2040s |

## 8. The three things to watch next

**1. SPARC, 2027.** The single most consequential scheduled event in the field. If a compact high-field tokamak produces Q > 1, the entire private magnetic-confinement thesis is validated and every schedule behind it compresses. If it fails or badly underperforms, the credibility damage extends across the sector and back into the public programs that have aligned their roadmaps to it. DOE has named it, in effect, as national **Milestone 1**.

**2. The NRC final rule, October 2026.** Expected but not guaranteed. It converts a proposed framework into settled law and unlocks state-level licensing for US developers.

**3. UNITY-3 and LIBRTI, late 2020s.** The first facilities capable of telling us whether tritium breeding works in a realistic blanket. This is the answer to the field's second-hardest question, and almost nobody outside the field is watching it.

---

## 9. An honest summary of where we are

**The plasma physics community has largely delivered.** Ignition is real and repeatable. Confinement is understood well enough to design machines that work. Stellarators have been rescued by computation. High-temperature superconductors have made compact high-field machines buildable. AI is now a working tool for plasma control rather than a promise.

**The nuclear engineering community has not yet been given the chance to deliver**, because the facilities it needs — a fusion-spectrum neutron source, an integrated breeding blanket test bed, a tritium plant at scale — are only now being funded and will not produce decisive data until the 2030s.

**The financial community has arrived early and in force**, at $14.24 billion and rising 69% a year, driven by an AI-shaped demand story rather than a technical one, and with an implied further requirement of over $100 billion that most current participants will not raise.

The result is a field in an unusual and somewhat precarious position: **its scientific risk is lower than at any point in its history, its engineering risk is almost entirely unretired, and its capital is being deployed on the assumption that the second will resolve as favourably as the first.**

The next eighteen months — SPARC's first plasma, the NRC's final rule, Helion's Orion, and the first real breeding-blanket data — will tell us whether that assumption was sound.
