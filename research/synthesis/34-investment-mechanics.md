# 34 — The Investment Mechanics

*Project Lux · How fusion deals are actually structured, and what the structures reveal*

---

## Scope

[[10-economics-and-markets|Document 10]] covered what fusion might cost and who might buy the electricity. This covers something narrower and more revealing: **the mechanics of how capital reaches fusion companies** — the instruments, the tranching, the valuations, and the public-market reception.

Deal structure is informative in a way that headline funding numbers are not. **How money is given tells you what the giver actually believes.**

---

## 1. The headline picture

| | |
|---|---|
| Cumulative funding | **$14.24 bn** |
| Raised in 12 months to July 2026 | **$4.48 bn** (+69%) |
| Private / public | **$13.26 bn / $0.98 bn** |
| Companies >$1 bn | 5 — CFS (~$4 bn after July), Helion ($1.5 bn), TAE ($1.5 bn), SHINE ($1.1 bn), [[pacific-fusion|Pacific Fusion]] ($1.0 bn) |
| Further capital each company estimates needing | **avg $2.7 bn, median $1.2 bn** |

The gap in that last row is the whole story. **56 companies at an average $2.7 bn further requirement implies well over $100 billion.** The sector has raised $14 bn in six years. The structures below are the market's attempt to bridge a gap of that size against technology risk nobody can underwrite conventionally.

---

## 2. The dominant innovation: [[milestone-tranching|milestone tranching]]

This is the most important structural development in fusion finance, and it originated in the public sector.

### The template — DOE's Milestone Program
Launched May 2023 with eight companies, explicitly modelled on **NASA's Commercial Orbital Transportation Services (COTS)** programme. Payment is made **on demonstrated milestones**, not on submitted invoices. The government specifies an outcome, the company decides how to reach it, and money moves only when the outcome is verified.

It has produced verifiable results: **[[thea-energy|Thea Energy]] became the first awardee to complete a full fusion pilot plant design review**, and **DOE approved Xcimer's "Athena" preconceptual design and technology roadmap** in August 2026.

### The migration into private capital
The model has now moved into venture financing. **Pacific Fusion secured a $1 billion Series A with milestone-based tranches** — capital committed up front, released against published technical milestones. The market observation is direct: *"investors are increasingly demanding milestones."*

**[[general-fusion|General Fusion]]'s LM26 programme is structured the same way**, with three defined technical milestones:
1. **1 keV** plasma heating
2. **10 keV**
3. **Achieving the Lawson criterion**

executing through 2028. (For calibration: LM26 has reached **0.72 keV**, so milestone one is close and milestone three is a very long way off.)

### Why this is a healthy development

It changes the incentive structure in three specific ways:

- **It prices technical risk explicitly.** A company that misses a milestone does not get the next tranche. That is a far sharper discipline than a valuation mark
- **It forces companies to publish falsifiable targets.** You cannot tranche against a vague objective
- **It reduces the cost of failure.** A company that stalls at milestone two has consumed one tranche, not a full round

It also, quietly, **transfers the sector's biggest problem into the open**. Every milestone schedule is a public statement about what a company thinks it can do and when — and those statements are now accumulating a track record.

## 3. The public markets, and what redemptions revealed

### General Fusion's de-SPAC — the numbers

The first fusion company to reach a public exchange, via merger with **Spring Valley Acquisition Corp. III**, completed 10 July 2026 and trading as **GFUZ**.

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Implied pro-forma equity value | **~US$1 billion** |
| Committed and oversubscribed PIPE | **US$107.7 million** |
| Spring Valley trust capital *(assuming no redemptions)* | **US$230 million** |
| **Actually raised in the transaction** | **US$127 million** |
| Cash to fund LM26 through end-2028 | **~US$150 million** |

**The important line is the discrepancy.** The deal was announced with $230 million of trust capital *assuming no redemptions*, and delivered $127 million.

In a SPAC, shareholders of the acquiring shell may redeem their shares for cash rather than participate in the merger. A high redemption rate means the public shareholders who could have owned a fusion company chose their money back instead.

**This is the first genuine public-market price signal fusion has ever received**, and it is a cautious one. The PIPE — institutional investors committing directly, oversubscribed at $107.7 million — was the strong part of the deal. The retail-adjacent trust capital largely walked.

### What listing changes

The FIA's assessment is that public markets bring capital *and* a different kind of scrutiny: *"the day-to-day financial progress of companies able to be tracked simply on a minute-to-minute ticker symbol."*

For a sector that has communicated through press releases for a decade, quarterly reporting obligations, audited statements and a live share price are a structural change. A missed milestone now has a price attached to it within minutes.

The FIA also noted a **second company in process** at the time of its 2026 report.

## 4. The investor base, and what its composition means

The 2026 investor list is the broadest in the sector's history, and the categories matter more than the names:

| Category | Examples | What their presence signals |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep-tech venture** | Founders Fund, Lowercarbon, Prime Movers Lab, Lightspeed, [[google|Google]] Ventures, Bessemer | Baseline risk appetite; been here since 2018 |
| **Crossover / public-market funds** | Fidelity, Morgan Stanley Counterpoint Global, Brevan Howard, Deerfield | Pre-IPO positioning; expect a liquidity event |
| **Pension funds** | **HostPlus**; the **$1 bn CFS raise led by pension capital** | **Infrastructure allocation.** These mandates buy long-duration assets, not experiments |
| **Sovereign / state innovation** | BPI France, SPRIND (Germany), IQT, Al Mirqab | Industrial policy expressed as equity |
| **Utilities and energy majors** | RWE, JERA, Kansai Electric, Eni | Strategic optionality on their own generation mix |
| **Industrial conglomerates** | Mitsubishi, Mitsui, Sumitomo Mitsui, Fujikura, NTT, Toyota Ventures | **Supply-chain positioning** — these firms expect to *build* fusion components |
| **Individuals** | Sam Altman, Stanley Druckenmiller | — |

**CFS raising $1 billion led by pension funds in July 2026 is the single most significant financing event in the sector's history**, and not because of the amount. Pension mandates are governed by rules that generally exclude pre-revenue technology risk. Their participation means at least some institutional allocators have reclassified fusion **from venture to infrastructure**.

The Japanese industrial participation is the other under-noticed signal. Mitsubishi, Mitsui, Sumitomo and Fujikura are not financial investors — they are the companies that would manufacture magnets, vacuum vessels and superconductors. **Strategic investment from your future suppliers is a different and arguably better validation than a venture round.**

## 5. The valley of death

Every capital-intensive technology has one; fusion's is unusually wide and unusually well-defined.

```
Stage 1 — Science           $10m–$100m   Venture. Works today
Stage 2 — Demonstration     $500m–$2bn   Venture + strategic + government. Working now
Stage 3 — First plant       $2bn–$6bn    ← THE VALLEY
Stage 4 — Fleet             $2bn+ each   Project finance, once FOAK de-risks it
```

**Stage 3 is the problem.** A first-of-a-kind plant is too large for venture capital, too risky for project finance, too novel for a utility balance sheet, and — with fusion outside Price-Anderson ([[23-standards-codes-liability|Document 23]]) — carries a liability profile suppliers have not priced.

The instruments being assembled to cross it:

- **Offtake agreements** — Google's 200 MW from [[arc|ARC]]; Helion's PPA with [[microsoft|Microsoft]]. These convert merchant price risk into contracted revenue, which is the precondition for project debt
- **Strategic equity from the offtaker** — Google invested in CFS as well as buying its power, which aligns interests through construction
- **Public-market capital** — General Fusion's route, at the cost of quarterly scrutiny and, evidently, redemption risk
- **Pension and infrastructure capital** — CFS's route, and the one with the deepest pools if fusion continues to be reclassified
- **Government cost-share** — the Milestone Program, Germany's €2 bn Action Plan, the UK's £2.5 bn
- **Siting at retired coal plants** — which reduces interconnection, permitting and community risk, all of which are financing risks ([[24-grid-integration-and-operations|Document 24]])

**Nobody has crossed the valley yet.** CFS's ARC, at over $2.5 billion, is the first serious attempt, and how it is financed will be more informative for the sector than how it performs.

## 6. Valuation — and its weak foundations

**Helion at $15.5 billion** is the sector's highest mark, on a company that has not demonstrated Q > 1.

Fusion valuations cannot rest on conventional methods. There are no cash flows, no comparables with revenue, and no historical base rate for success. In practice they rest on some combination of:

- **Option value** — a low probability of an enormous total addressable market. Global electricity is a multi-trillion-dollar annual market; a small probability of a meaningful share is worth a lot
- **Scarcity** — a limited number of credible teams, and more capital wanting exposure than there are companies to absorb it
- **Milestone progress** — increasingly the anchor, which is the point of tranching
- **Comparable rounds** — circular, and the mechanism by which sector-wide marks move together

The uncomfortable implication: **fusion valuations are highly correlated.** They rest on a shared belief that the technology works, and they will re-rate together on shared evidence.

**[[sparc|SPARC]]'s 2027 result is therefore a sector-wide valuation event**, not a CFS-specific one. A clean Q > 1 re-rates everyone. A visible failure re-rates everyone, including companies pursuing entirely unrelated approaches — which is a genuine and underappreciated risk for the [[stellarator]], mirror and pulsed-power companies whose physics has nothing to do with SPARC's.

## 7. Assessment

**Milestone tranching is the sector's most important financial innovation**, and it came from government. It prices technical risk, forces falsifiable public targets, and caps the cost of failure. Its spread from DOE into Pacific Fusion's Series A and General Fusion's LM26 programme is a sign of a maturing market.

**Pension capital entering is a stronger signal than the $4.48 bn headline.** It means fusion has been reclassified by at least some allocators from a venture bet to an infrastructure asset, which unlocks a far deeper pool.

**The SPAC redemption tells the other half of the story.** Announced with $230 million of trust capital, delivered $127 million. Institutional PIPE investors committed and were oversubscribed; the broader public market largely took its money back. **Fusion's institutional support is currently much stronger than its retail support**, which is the reverse of most hyped technologies and is arguably healthier.

**The valley of death is unbridged and the bridge is being built out of offtake agreements.** Whether a first-of-a-kind fusion plant can be financed at all is a question that will be answered by ARC's capital structure, not by its plasma.

**And the correlation risk is real.** One machine, in one building in Massachusetts, in 2027, carries the mark-to-market of an entire sector — including the parts of it that have nothing to do with tokamaks.


## The public half of the capital stack

This document covers private-market mechanics. [[44-state-capital-and-the-public-balance-sheet]] covers the other half: public money
acting as an **investor** rather than a research funder. The asymmetry is sharp — Chinese public
mobilisation of **$6.5–13 bn** (2023–2025) against a US Fusion Energy Sciences budget of
**$806 m** for 2026, while private capital runs the other way (53% of ~$15 bn to US companies, 34%
to China). Pension and sovereign funds entering private rounds directly is the newer development,
supplying capital whose duration matches the asset in a way ten-year venture funds do not.
