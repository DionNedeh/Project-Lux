The constraint nobody priced.

> The workforce needs of the nascent fusion industry are growing at a rate that **academic workforce development programs are not currently able to match.**

## Findings

- **A contraction in PhD admissions in the 2024–2025 cycle**, with universities unable to sustain previous intake
- Projected consequence: **a shortage of principal investigators and lead engineers in the early 2030s** — *precisely when commercial fusion plants are expected to come online*
- **International students comprise up to 72% of graduate populations** in relevant fields; that supply line is not being replenished
- The sector employed roughly **2,400 people in 2023, nearly 40% holding PhDs** — a doctoral density that takes eight to ten years to reproduce and cannot be scaled by hiring

Meanwhile **China is reported to be investing $6.5 bn and training 10,000 fusion engineers.**

## Responses

**[[pppl]] launched a four-year registered apprenticeship for technicians**, open to anyone with a high school diploma — arguably the most important item in the sector's workforce response, because fusion does not only need PhDs. The inaugural **Fusion Innovation Bootcamp** ran 15–26 June 2026 at Tennessee State University, themed *Fusion Supply Chain and Workforce Development*.
