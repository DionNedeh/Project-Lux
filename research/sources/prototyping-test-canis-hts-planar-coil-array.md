This peer-reviewed engineering study reports the manufacture and cryogenic testing of [[canis]], a 3 × 3 array built to test [[thea-energy|Thea Energy]]'s planar-coil approach to the [[stellarator]]. The full text inspected here is the 14-page author-hosted manuscript; the journal record is IEEE Transactions on Applied Superconductivity 35(7), article 3801014, DOI 10.1109/TASC.2025.3594533.

The team manufactured **nine [[rebco]] shaping coils from 45 double-pancake windings**, using tape from three suppliers. Its production line reached a final takt time of roughly **half a day per double pancake** and total production time of **no more than three days per double pancake**. The complete array operated at **20 K**. Two static, stellarator-relevant field patterns measured on a plane 25 cm from the array had RMS errors of **0.56% and 0.60%** relative to predictions. For each pattern, **7,578** uncertainty-perturbed simulations yielded 95th-percentile RMS errors of **0.91% and 0.94%**.

That is a demonstrated component result: repeatable planar-coil manufacture, multi-supplier integration and closed-loop field control inside the study's ±1% target. It does **not** demonstrate a stellarator plasma. Canis was a flat nine-coil array; it could not create net toroidal flux, closed flux surfaces or quasisymmetry, and it did not reproduce full [[eos|Eos]] forces, currents or systems density. Transient field control, several quench scenarios, defect robustness and denser field-shaping-unit integration remained future work. The source therefore retires part of the coil-manufacturing risk without validating Eos or the planned Helios power plant.
