This scoping review asks how much social-science evidence actually supports confident claims about fusion’s public acceptability. Its published sample contains only **14 research articles from 2005–2025**, concentrated in Europe and the United States. That is a small and geographically narrow base for a technology discussed as a global energy option.

Across the included literature, awareness is generally low and expressed attitudes are often positive, but the review characterises them as **tentative and weakly held**. Prior attitudes towards fission, affective responses, perceived risks and benefits, and trust in institutions are recurring predictors. Information can shift responses, but the direction and durability depend on what is said, who says it and which trade-offs become salient.

The review therefore undercuts both a promotional claim—“the public supports fusion”—and a fatalistic opposite—“nuclear stigma makes acceptance impossible”. Current opinion is better understood as provisional. Many respondents have not yet encountered a concrete site, bill impact, construction programme, waste plan or accident-governance proposal.

The source supports [[42-public-acceptability-siting-social-licence|Document 42]]’s distinction between broad approval and project-level social licence. It also exposes a research gap: cross-sectional polling cannot substitute for longitudinal work around real host communities and institutions.
