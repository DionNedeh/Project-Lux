# Powering U.S. Innovation: The Need for Federal Investment in Fusion Infrastructure

A CSIS *Perspectives on Innovation* analysis arguing that US public fusion investment is
insufficient relative to China's. It is the corpus's first source treating **public money as an
investor** rather than as a grant-making programme, and it anchors
[[44-state-capital-and-the-public-balance-sheet]].

## Figures it supplies

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| DOE Fusion Energy Sciences, 2020–2023 total | $2.34 bn |
| FES budget, 2025 | $790 m |
| FES budget, 2026 | $806 m (+2%) |
| Share of FES 2020–2023 directed to commercialisation | ~1.2% |
| Chinese public fusion funds mobilised, 2023–2025 | ≥$6.5 bn (some estimates to $13 bn) |
| Ratio, Chinese public to US FES over that period | ~3× |
| Global private fusion investment as of 2025 | ~$15 bn |
| Share to US-based companies | 53% |
| Share to China | 34% |
| Federal investment in the National Ignition Facility | $3.5 bn |
| Recommended one-time public increase | $10 bn |

It also records that [[us-doe]] has newly established an Office of Fusion, and names
[[nif|NIF]], PPPL and DIII-D as federally built infrastructure that private
companies now depend on.

## How to read it

**This is advocacy, and should be cited as such.** CSIS is arguing a policy position — that the
United States is underinvesting — and the $10 bn figure is a recommendation attributed to
unnamed "industry experts", not a costed programme. The budget lines and the Chinese comparison are
the durable content; the framing is not neutral.

The Chinese figure carries a wide band ($6.5–13 bn) and the source does not resolve it. Per
`CLAUDE.md` §7.4 the range is preserved rather than collapsed to a point estimate.

The 53%/34% private-capital split is broadly consistent with the
[[fia-global-fusion-industry-report-2026|FIA 2026 report]] already in the corpus, though the two use
different totals and different cut-off dates; they are not directly comparable.
