The demand-side case, and the reason the fusion funding curve turned upward in 2025–26 rather than in 2023.

## The numbers

- AI data-centre demand: **460 TWh (2024) → ~1,300 TWh (2035)**
- A single hyperscale AI facility draws **300–500 MW** — comparable to a mid-sized city
- As of May 2026, hyperscalers had committed **over 9.8 GW across 13 nuclear projects**

## Why fusion fits this demand specifically

Firm 24/7 baseload · very high power density on a small footprint · carbon-free · long-term price certainty with negligible fuel exposure · siteable near load, avoiding transmission queues · politically easier than fission under the byproduct-material framework.

The [[fusion-industry-association]]'s own foreword names it: *"the last year has added an important new driver: the energy demand of artificial intelligence."*

## The counterargument

Hyperscalers need power **now**, and fusion will not deliver at scale this decade. The PPAs from [[microsoft]] and [[google]] are best understood as **option purchases and strategic signalling** — cheap relative to those balance sheets, valuable if they work. That does not make them unimportant: even as options they do the financing work that makes project debt possible.
