Capturing charged-particle energy as electricity **without a thermal cycle** — bypassing the Carnot limit entirely.

A conventional fusion plant is a large thermal power station: 35–45% conversion efficiency, set by coolant outlet temperature, which is set by the structural material's limit, which is set by irradiation behaviour. The whole chain from neutron damage to electricity price runs through that one link. See [[recirculating-power]].

DEC removes the chain. It is only available to configurations with an open field line or a pulsed compression:

- **[[magnetic-mirror|Mirrors]]** — particles escaping the ends are decelerated against an electrostatic field. **[[realta-fusion]] demonstrated DEC on [[wham]] on 19 June 2026** — the first commercial fusion company to convert plasma energy directly to electricity
- **[[helion-energy]]** — the entire architecture is inductive recovery from an expanding compressed [[field-reversed-configuration]]. No steam cycle at all
- **[[aneutronic-fusion]]** would make DEC dramatically more valuable, since nearly all the energy is in charged particles

This is the strongest single argument for the non-tokamak concepts, and it is why several target **industrial process heat or off-grid power first** — markets where the thermal-cycle penalty does not apply.
