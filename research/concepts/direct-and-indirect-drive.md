The two principal ways to couple a laser to an ICF capsule.

## Indirect drive — [[nif]]

Beams enter a centimetre-scale **hohlraum** of gold or depleted uranium and heat its inner wall, which re-radiates a near-Planckian X-ray bath at a few hundred eV. Those X-rays ablate the capsule.

- **Advantage:** the X-ray bath is highly uniform, relaxing the beam-symmetry requirement enormously
- **Disadvantage:** only ~10–15% of laser energy reaches the capsule. Fatal for a power plant's energy balance

## Direct drive — [[omega-laser]], and most startups

Beams illuminate the capsule directly.

- **Advantage:** several times better coupling — the only route with plausible power-plant economics
- **Disadvantage:** demands extreme beam-to-beam power balance and pointing accuracy, and is fully exposed to laser-plasma instabilities

OMEGA (60 beams, 30 kJ) is far smaller than NIF but has produced the best hydrodynamic scaling results in direct drive.

See also [[fast-ignition]], which decouples compression from ignition entirely.
