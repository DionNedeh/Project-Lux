The field's single most useful figure of merit: **peak ion density × peak ion temperature × energy confinement time**.

For D-T ignition the idealised minimum is roughly **3 × 10²¹ keV·m⁻³·s**, occurring near T ≈ 14 keV. Real plasmas with peaked profiles and impurities need **3–5× more**.

It matters because it collapses three separately-tradeable quantities into one number, which is why [[lawson-criterion|Lawson's criteria]] can be plotted on a single axis and machines of wildly different design compared directly.

## The record that has stood since 1995

| Machine | Year | Triple product |
|---|---|---|
| [[zeta]] | 1957 | 9.0 × 10¹⁴ |
| [[tftr]] | **1995** | **7.9 × 10²⁰** ← highest ever achieved |
| [[jet]] (DTE3) | 2023 | 1.4 × 10²⁰ |
| [[sparc]] | 2027 *(projected)* | **6.2 × 10²¹** |
| [[iter]] | 2039 *(projected)* | 7.4 × 10²¹ |

**No tokamak has beaten TFTR's 1995 value in thirty-one years.** Not a physics plateau — no machine capable of exceeding it was built between TFTR's shutdown in 1997 and the 2020s. See [[26-appendix-quantitative-data]] for the full dataset and [[39-the-discourse]] for why this reframes the "thirty years away" complaint.

Beware: a temperature record without a confinement time is **not** a triple product. That conflation produced the [[zeta]] retraction of 1958.
