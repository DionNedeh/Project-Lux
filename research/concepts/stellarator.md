A toroidal device in which the **entire confining field, including the rotational transform, is generated by external coils**. No plasma current is required.

Invented by **Lyman Spitzer at Princeton in 1951** (Project Matterhorn). Abandoned worldwide within two years of the 1969 [[t-3]] results. Now, in 2026, holding the world [[triple-product]] record for pulses over 10 seconds, and **outnumbering tokamaks among private fusion companies 8 to 5**.

## Why it is the better reactor concept

- **No [[disruption|disruptions]]** — the tokamak's principal failure mode simply does not exist
- **Intrinsically steady-state** — no transformer, no dwell, no thermal cycling, no storage system. See [[24-grid-integration-and-operations]]
- **No [[greenwald-density-limit]]** of the tokamak type

## Why it is the worse construction project

[[wendelstein-7-x]]'s 50 non-planar superconducting coils are shaped to roughly **1 mm tolerance**. Manufacturing cost and schedule risk are severe, and maintenance access through a 3D coil set is genuinely difficult — the strongest practical argument against the concept.

[[thea-energy|Thea Energy]] attacks that constraint by replacing bespoke non-planar coils with many individually powered planar coils. Its [[canis]] prototype demonstrated manufacture and closed-loop control of a flat nine-coil [[rebco]] array within 1% of predicted static field. It did not form closed flux surfaces or a plasma, so [[eos|Eos]] remains an integrated-machine test rather than a proven reactor architecture. Evidence and limits: [[prototyping-test-canis-hts-planar-coil-array]].

The architecture scaled to a plant is [[helios|Helios]], whose published preconceptual design quantifies two of the concept's standing claims: [[recirculating-power|recirculating power]] near **15%** of generated electricity rather than a tokamak's 20–40%, since an ignited stellarator needs under 1 MW of heating; and no thermal-storage system, because output never pulses. It also claims the first stellarator equilibrium explicitly designed around a tokamak-like X-point [[divertor]], addressing the unsolved exhaust problem in [[wendelstein-7-x|W7-X]]'s island-divertor line. All of it is simulation — see [[overview-of-the-helios-design]].

## What changed

**Computation.** An unoptimised 3D field has catastrophic neoclassical transport. Once fields could be numerically optimised for [[quasi-symmetry]] or quasi-isodynamicity, the problem became tractable by design.
