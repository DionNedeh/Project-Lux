The oldest fusion idea: run a current through a plasma column and let its own magnetic field pinch it. Elegant — it requires **no external field coils at all**.

## Why it failed in the 1950s

Violent instability to **sausage (m=0)** and **kink (m=1)** modes. Any small constriction increases the local field, which increases the pinch, which increases the constriction — collapse in microseconds.

Worse, the instabilities accelerate ions to high energy, producing **beam-target neutrons that look thermonuclear and are not**. This is precisely the error that destroyed [[zeta]]'s 1958 claim.

## The revival

**[[zap-energy]]** argues that a **sheared axial flow** stabilises exactly these modes, permitting a pinch with no external coils. It has reported electron temperatures above **1 keV** in FuZE.

If sheared-flow stabilisation holds at reactor-relevant currents, the resulting machine is radically simpler and cheaper than anything else in fusion. That is a large "if" against seventy years of contrary evidence — which is what makes it interesting.

Separately, **[[z-machine]]** at Sandia uses pulsed-power Z-pinch physics for [[magneto-inertial-fusion]].
