**Nobody knows what fusion electricity will cost, and the credible range spans more than an order of magnitude.**

A 2026 *Nature Energy* analysis compiled first-of-a-kind capital cost estimates and found **$1,400 to $43,000 per kilowatt** — a thirty-fold spread. That is not a forecast; it is an admission the technology is too immature for the question to be answerable.

## The most rigorous independent analysis

Böhnlein, Böse, von Hirschhausen, Kemfert and Wimmers (TU Berlin / DIW), a stochastic Monte Carlo harmonisation of **56 references** — see [[too-cheap-to-meter-stochastic-fusion-costs-2026]]. Mature-technology mean LCOE:

| Line | Mean LCOE |
|---|---|
| Magnetic confinement | **$114.6/MWh** |
| Inertial confinement | **$110.3/MWh** |
| Magneto-inertial | **$143.9/MWh** |

Their conclusion, quoted: *"these projected values are rather optimistic... We therefore urge policymakers to caution when potential fusion developers refer to the potential economic competitiveness of fusion power plants."* Reaching them implies **learning rates above 30%** — solar PV achieved 20–25%; Western fission achieved *negative* learning.

## Reference points

- **NASEM threshold:** pilot plant overnight cost **< $5 bn**. [[manta]] claims $3.4 bn
- **[[arc]]**: ~400 MWe for **>$2.5 bn** ≈ **$6,250/kW** — the field's first real cost datum
- **Balance-of-plant floor:** a 2 GWe p-¹¹B plant with a *free* fusion core still costs **$7.1/MWh**. The floor is not zero

The dominant swing variables are [[availability]] and cost of capital, not physics.
