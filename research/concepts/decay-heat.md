Residual heat released by radioactive decay after a reactor stops. It is the single clearest
structural difference between fusion's safety case and fission's, and most of fusion's regulatory
argument rests on it.

## Why it dominates fission's risk

Fission's worst accidents were decay-heat accidents. At Three Mile Island and Fukushima the reactor
**shut down correctly** — and the residual heat from fission-product decay melted the core anyway,
because cooling failed. A fission core cannot be walked away from at shutdown; it must be actively
cooled for an extended period, and that requirement drives much of the plant's safety architecture,
its emergency systems and its cost.

## Why fusion's is different

Fusion's radioactive inventory is [[activation-products|activation products]] in the structure, not
fission products in fuel. The resulting decay heat is far lower, and the IAEA review behind
[[43-fusion-waste-classification-and-disposal]] identifies "lower decay heat removal" as one of the
principal benefits of reduced-activation materials.

Low enough, in the designs assessed so far, that **passive cooling suffices**: the structure can
radiate and conduct its residual heat away without powered systems. There is no fusion equivalent
of a core melt driven by loss of cooling, because there is no core to melt and not enough residual
power to do it.

## What it buys, and what it does not

This is the technical basis for regulating fusion as a radiological facility rather than as a
reactor — the distinction traced through [[11-policy-regulation-safety]] and
[[41-fusion-regulation-beyond-the-united-states]]. Fusion is regulated on the radioactive materials
it handles, principally tritium and activation products, rather than on accident scenarios
involving criticality and decay heat.

It does not make fusion hazard-free. A plant still holds large tritium inventories, activated
structures, high magnetic and cryogenic stored energies, hazardous coolants such as
[[lithium-lead-eutectic|PbLi]], [[beryllium]] dust and intense neutron fields. Decay heat removes
one specific accident class; it does not remove the rest, and
[[33-safety-environment-decommissioning]] treats those directly.
