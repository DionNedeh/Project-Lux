One of the few fusion markets already generating revenue.

## Molybdenum-99

**Mo-99** decays to **technetium-99m**, used in roughly 40 million diagnostic procedures a year — the workhorse of nuclear medicine. Historically produced in a handful of ageing research reactors using **highly enriched uranium**: a supply chain that is both fragile and a proliferation concern.

**[[shine-technologies]]** is building a non-reactor route using accelerator-driven fusion neutron sources irradiating a low-enriched uranium solution. **NNSA selected SHINE for $32 million**; the facility is ~75% complete. A proposed Medicare rule adds a **$10 payment for radiopharmaceuticals from domestically produced Mo-99** from 1 January 2026.

## Therapeutic radionuclides

**[[astral-systems]]** has validated production of **Cu-64, Cu-67, I-131, Tb-161, Sc-44 and Sc-47**. Tb-161 and Sc-47 in particular are emerging therapeutic radionuclides with real clinical demand and constrained supply.

## Boron Neutron Capture Therapy

BNCT exploits `n + ¹⁰B` capture in tumour cells, releasing short-range high-LET particles that kill the cell they were born in and spare its neighbour. Particularly suited to **infiltrating tumours such as glioblastoma**.

Historically it required a reactor. Since 2014, **accelerator-based epithermal neutron sources sitable in hospitals** have been built by Sumitomo Heavy Industries, Hitachi, Mitsubishi and Neutron Therapeutics. **[[tae-technologies]]** entered via TAE Life Sciences.
