**The hardest unsolved problem in fusion**, and the reason fission data cannot qualify fusion materials.

The 14.1 MeV D-T neutron sits above the ~3 MeV threshold for **(n,α) and (n,p) transmutation** in structural metals. That produces **helium and hydrogen inside the metal lattice**. Helium is insoluble; it migrates to grain boundaries and forms bubbles.

## The ratio that matters

| Spectrum | Helium production |
|---|---|
| Fission | **~0.1–1 appm He/dpa** |
| **Fusion** | **~10–15 appm He/dpa** |

An order of magnitude more helium for the same [[displacements-per-atom|displacement damage]].

## Consequences

- **High-temperature helium embrittlement** — grain-boundary bubbles cause intergranular fracture with essentially no ductility
- **Void swelling** — helium stabilises voids, causing dimensional change of several percent
- **DBTT shift** — ductile materials become brittle, sometimes by hundreds of degrees

It also breaks **welding**: above roughly 1 appm helium, conventional fusion welding of irradiated steel becomes unreliable, which is why remote in-vessel replacement is unqualified. See [[29-remote-handling-and-maintenance]].

**There is no operating fusion-spectrum neutron source anywhere in the world.**
