John Lawson's 1955 analysis at Harwell (declassified 1957) established the minimum conditions for a fusion plasma to produce more energy than it costs to sustain.

**Lawson parameter (D-T ignition):** `n·τ_E ≳ 1.5 × 10²⁰ m⁻³·s`, minimum near T ≈ 25 keV.
**As a [[triple-product]]:** `n·T·τ_E ≳ 3 × 10²¹ m⁻³·keV·s`, minimum near T ≈ 14 keV.

The balance being struck: fusion power deposited in charged products (for D-T, the alpha's **20%** — the neutron's 80% escapes immediately) must exceed conduction plus bremsstrahlung losses.

## Terminology

Wurzel and Hsu note it should be **"criteria", plural**: there are two separate requirements, one on `n·τ_E` and one on temperature. And "the Lawson criterion" is usually taken to mean [[ignition]], whereas Lawson himself described criteria for *a useful reactor* — which he put at a gain of about 2.

For [[inertial-confinement]] the criteria are reformulated in terms of [[areal-density]], because confinement time is set by disassembly rather than transport.
