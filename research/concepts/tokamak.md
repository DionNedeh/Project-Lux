The dominant magnetic confinement configuration. A toroidal chamber in which a strong **toroidal field** from external coils (5–12 T) is combined with a **poloidal field** generated by a plasma current of several megamperes, driven inductively by a central solenoid acting as a transformer primary.

Invented by **Igor Tamm and Andrei Sakharov** (USSR, c. 1950–51); first working device **T-1**, built by Natan Yavlinsky, 1958. The name is a Russian acronym for *toroidal chamber with magnetic coils*.

## Why it won

The 1968 [[t-3]] results — 1 keV electron temperature, confinement an order of magnitude better than anything Western — were confirmed in 1969 by a British team from Culham who carried a laser Thomson scattering system to Moscow. Within two years essentially every fusion laboratory in the world had converted. Historians call it the **great tokamak stampede**.

## Strengths and the structural weakness

Best-understood scheme by a wide margin; highest [[triple-product|triple products]] ever achieved; axisymmetric, so good fast-alpha confinement; [[h-mode]] gives a factor-of-two confinement bonus for free.

Against that: **[[disruption|disruptions]]** — the plasma current is both the confinement mechanism and a stored energy reservoir — and **inherently pulsed operation** unless a high [[bootstrap-current]] fraction can be sustained.
