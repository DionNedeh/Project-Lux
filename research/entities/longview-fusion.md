California company proposing a laser inertial-fusion power plant derived from the [[nif|National Ignition Facility]] architecture. Its claim is not that it must rediscover target ignition, but that it can turn laboratory gain into a repeatable, maintainable power system.

The [[fia-global-fusion-industry-report-2026]] records **$12 million declared funding** in the corpus’s company comparison. The company profile says it is developing utility-scale grid plants, behind-the-meter supply and commodity-production applications, and reports additions to its intellectual-property portfolio across fusion technology and plant configuration.

The distinction between inherited and unproven performance is essential. NIF has demonstrated target gain, not a laser wall-plug cycle, high repetition rate, target manufacture and injection, chamber clearing, blanket operation or Q_eng > 1. Longview’s NIF lineage therefore supplies a demonstrated physics starting point, while the plant it proposes remains designed and announced.
