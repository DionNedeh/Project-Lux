Toronto stellarator company founded by Spencer Pitcher, Amitava Bhattacharjee, Mike Zarnstorff and Ali Lodi. Its stated intermediate objective is a deuterium–tritium device demonstrating net fusion-energy gain before a commercial plant.

The [[fia-global-fusion-industry-report-2026]] lists a lithium neutron blanket, a projected **Q_sci > 1 demonstration in 2031**, engineering net energy and a first pilot plant in the 2030s, and an announced commercial output of 230 MWe. These are company targets; the source reports no achieved fusion performance and gives no declared-funding figure.

Named collaborators include Princeton University, Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory, Savannah River National Laboratory, Canadian Nuclear Laboratories, Kinectrics, Hatch, Max Planck IPP and the Simons Foundation’s Hidden Symmetries collaboration. That network connects Stellarex to the modern [[quasi-symmetry]] and optimisation lineage, but it does not remove the common reactor-level dependencies on tritium breeding, materials qualification and maintainability documented in [[30-power-plant-designs-compared]].
