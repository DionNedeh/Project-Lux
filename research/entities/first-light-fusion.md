Oxford. **The clearest case so far of a well-funded fusion company concluding its own approach would not reach a plant.**

First Light spent years developing projectile-driven inertial fusion: fire a hypervelocity projectile at a target containing an "amplifier" structure that focuses the resulting shock. It achieved real technical results, including validated fusion neutrons.

**In 2025 it pivoted away from being a fusion plant developer**, refocusing on **licensing its shock-amplifier technology** for other applications. It has continued to raise — a **£25 m first close led by East X Ventures** through the Starmaker One fusion fund in 2026 — but no longer claims it will build a power station.

This is the healthy version of failure: a competent team assessed its own approach against a credible timeline and redirected to what its technology was actually good for. The FIA notes **three companies dropped out** of the 2026 survey, and its CEO states the general expectation plainly: *"few expect there to be 56 companies"* in a commercial fusion industry. See [[21-the-graveyard]].
