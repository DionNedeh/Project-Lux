Kearny, New Jersey. Formerly Princeton Stellarators.

**The distinctive idea:** rather than complex 3D coils, Thea uses **arrays of simple planar coils** whose individually controlled currents synthesise the required 3D field. [[canis]] demonstrated the component-level premise: nine planar [[rebco]] coils operated at 20 K and controlled two static field patterns with 0.56% and 0.60% RMS error. That is not yet an integrated stellarator result; [[eos]] remains planned.

## 2026

- **First [[us-doe]] Milestone Program awardee to complete a full fusion pilot plant design review** — a genuine sector first. See [[milestone-tranching]]
- **$20 m [[arpa-e]] SCALEUP award (July 2026)** to establish **the first US production line for modular [[rebco]] HTS magnets**
- Series B extension (August 2026) on top of a $100 m round led by Thomas Tull's US Innovative Technology Fund
- Stellarator **digital twin** partnership with **NVIDIA, Synopsys, Argonne and Princeton** — notable for including an EDA company, signalling a chip-industry approach to design verification. See [[19-simulation-codes-nuclear-data]]

Machines: [[canis]] (magnet-array prototype, built) → [[eos|Eos]] (planned demonstrator) → [[helios|Helios]] (planned power plant). Evidence and limits: [[prototyping-test-canis-hts-planar-coil-array]] for the hardware, [[overview-of-the-helios-design]] for the plant design.

The design review noted above now has a published output: a Fusion Engineering and Design special issue on Helios, whose overview paper sets a **1,094 MW** thermal, **~390 MWe** net design point with a full [[balance-of-plant|balance-of-plant]] accounting. It remains a preconceptual design — Thea's only hardware result is still Canis.
