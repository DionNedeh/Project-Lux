The **Kazakhstan Tokamak for Material Testing (KTM)**, designed around plasma–material interaction in the [[divertor]] region rather than as a general-purpose confinement machine.

The project source named in [[15-references]] and the Round 3 research notes describe KTM as reproducing conditions expected near ITER’s divertor as closely as its designers considered then possible. That is a design claim, not an independently established equivalence to [[iter]]. Its value is the deliberate focus on wall and divertor exposure—one of the constraints ranked above plasma science in [[09-engineering-challenges]].

KTM also represents a route by which a country outside the eight largest fusion programmes can contribute a specialised facility. Kazakhstan participates in ITER through Russia and has inherited nuclear-technical institutions from the Soviet period. The machine’s materials-testing purpose is demonstrated by its design and programme; the current corpus does not establish a qualified fusion-neutron spectrum or reactor-level displacement-damage capability, so neither is claimed.
