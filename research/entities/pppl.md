Founded as **Project Matterhorn** in 1951, when Lyman Spitzer invented the [[stellarator]]. Home of **[[tftr]]**, which still holds the [[triple-product]] record set in 1995.

Current work spans stellarator theory and optimisation, spherical tokamaks (NSTX-U), AI plasma control, and computation ([[19-simulation-codes-nuclear-data|XGC]]).

**Notably, PPPL launched a four-year registered apprenticeship programme for technicians, open to anyone with a high school diploma** — arguably the most important workforce initiative in the sector, because fusion does not only need PhDs. It needs vacuum technicians, cryogenic operators, precision welders, remote-handling operators and radiological control staff. See [[27-people-institutions-public-trust]].
