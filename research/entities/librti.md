**Lithium Breeding Tritium Innovation** — a **£220 million** [[ukaea]] facility, described as a **global first-of-a-kind for tritium fuel technology**.

Total investment £220 m across 2025/26 – 2029/30, within the UK's £2.5 bn five-year fusion commitment.

Together with [[unity-3]], it is where [[tritium-breeding-ratio]] moves from calculation to measurement.

## The accountancy instruments

Added 2026-10-19. LIBRTI is not only breeding a ratio — with [[ukaea|UKAEA]]'s **Tritium Fuel Cycle
division** it is building the **measurements** that [[tritium-accountancy|tritium accountancy]]
currently lacks ([[thalia-hera-ukaea-tritium-accountancy-2026]]). Two named projects:

- **HERA** — real-time tritium analysis in **gas streams**, by mass spectrometry and Raman
  spectrometry, quantifying H₂, D₂ and T₂ from the limit of detection to 100%, with a palladium
  catalyst for semi-quantification of HD, HT and DT, and a glovebox that generates its own
  calibration standards by PVTc.
- **THALIA** — accountancy for tritium **trapped in liquid breeders**: FLiBe first, then liquid
  lithium and **[[lithium-lead-eutectic|LiPb]]**. Sub-projects **ERATO** (liquid scintillation
  counting of tritiated lithium salts) and **CLIO** (ICP-MS for hydrogen isotopes, a technique not
  previously developed for atoms this small).

**Both are at low technology readiness level**, with tritium **speciation in the breeder unknown**.

That matters for the assessment below: measuring tritium *in* a breeder is a harder and less mature
problem than breeding it, and no method currently exists for quantifying blanket tritium production
at all. **LIBRTI is addressing a gap that the vault's other sources only name.**

Added 2026-11-02. LIBRTI's conference programme is also where [[ukaea|UKAEA]] set out its
**[[vanadium-alloys|V-4Cr-4Ti]] strategy** ([[ukaea-2026-vanadium-strategy]]), naming a
V44/liquid-lithium self-cooled blanket as its long-term bet. That places the structural alloy and
the breeder in the same programme — appropriate, since lithium strips oxygen from vanadium while
carbon and nitrogen in the lithium diffuse into it, so neither can be qualified independently of
the other.

**Strategically, this is one of the best-chosen public investments in fusion anywhere.** The UK did not attempt to compete on machine size; it invested in **the two things nobody else was adequately funding** — lithium breeding and, through [[h3at]], tritium handling.
