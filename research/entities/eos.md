[[thea-energy|Thea Energy]]'s planned integrated deuterium-deuterium planar-coil [[stellarator]] and neutron-source demonstrator, intended to precede the larger deuterium-tritium [[helios|Helios]] power-plant concept.

Instead of winding a small number of bespoke three-dimensional modular coils, Eos is designed to use arrays of individually powered planar coils to synthesise the required three-dimensional magnetic field. The manufacturing claim has partial hardware support: [[canis]], a flat nine-coil [[rebco]] array, was manufactured and operated at 20 K and controlled two static field patterns within 1% of prediction.

The preconceptual design of the plant Eos is meant to precede is now published ([[overview-of-the-helios-design]]), which fixes what Eos must eventually retire: a two-field-period quasi-axisymmetric equilibrium with 324 individually controlled shaping coils, and a tokamak-like X-point [[divertor]] never yet built in a stellarator.

What remains designed rather than demonstrated is the integrated machine. Canis produced no toroidal flux, closed magnetic surfaces, quasisymmetry or plasma, and did not reproduce Eos-scale forces, currents or component density. Eos's plasma performance and neutron-source role therefore remain planned. See [[prototyping-test-canis-hts-planar-coil-array]] for the component result and its limits.
