The largest and, on most measures, the most credible private fusion company. Spun out of MIT's Plasma Science and Fusion Center in 2018 to commercialise [[rebco]] high-temperature superconductors, on the thesis of [[b4-scaling]].

**~$4 bn raised** (FIA-declared $3 bn plus the July 2026 $1 bn raise). ~1,000 employees, Devens, Massachusetts.

## Why it is taken seriously

- **Published its full physics basis openly** — a seven-paper *Journal of Plasma Physics* special issue in 2020, inviting exactly the scrutiny most startups avoid
- **De-risked its central claim first**: the **20 tesla model coil test of September 2021** proved the magnet before the machine was designed around it
- Collaborators: MIT, Brookhaven, Columbia, [[max-planck-ipp]], NREL, NVIDIA, Dominion Energy

## Status, August 2026

- **[[sparc]] ~80% assembled**; first plasma and scientific breakeven targeted **2027**
- **[[arc]]** permitted at Chesterfield County, Virginia; ~400 MWe; >$2.5 bn; early 2030s
- **$1 billion raised from pension funds** in July 2026 — the first time institutional retirement capital treated fusion as infrastructure. Largest single-company raise in the sector at **$863 m** in the FIA survey year
- **Now sells magnets to other fusion companies**, starting with [[realta-fusion]] (April 2026)

That last item is strategically the most interesting: CFS is becoming a **supplier as well as a developer**, which builds the HTS supply chain the whole industry needs and hedges its own single-machine risk.


**Pension and sovereign capital.** CFS was reported in July 2026 to have raised **$1 bn** from
pension funds and sovereign wealth funds, described as a first for the sector. Single-outlet trade
reporting, and announced rather than verified as deployed; see [[44-state-capital-and-the-public-balance-sheet]] for why the duration of
that capital matters more than its size.
