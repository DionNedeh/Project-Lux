192 laser beams, frequency-tripled to **351 nm**, delivering ~2 MJ in a shaped nanosecond pulse at peak powers near **500 terawatts**. Completed March 2009 at [[llnl]].

**Its primary mission is stockpile stewardship** — validating weapons physics without nuclear testing. Fusion energy is a secondary benefit of a weapons-physics facility, which explains its design priorities (single-shot, diagnostically rich, efficiency-indifferent). See [[37-military-dual-use-security]].

## The ignition record

| Date | Laser in | Yield | Target gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | 1.8 MJ | ~0.02 MJ | 0.01 — National Ignition Campaign **fails** |
| 8 Aug 2021 | 1.9 MJ | 1.35 MJ | 0.69 — [[burning-plasma]] |
| **5 Dec 2022** | **2.05 MJ** | **3.15 MJ** | **1.5 — first [[ignition]] in history** |
| 2023 | 2.05 MJ | 3.88 MJ | 1.89 |
| 2024 | 2.2 MJ | 5.2 MJ | 2.36 |
| **7 Apr 2025** | **2.08 MJ** | **8.6 MJ** | **4.13 — record** |
| 20 Jun 2026 | ~2.08 MJ | 7.9 ± 0.4 MJ | ~3.8 — **11th ignition** |

> [!warning] Target gain is not net energy
> NIF's laser draws roughly **300–400 MJ from the grid** to deliver 2.08 MJ on target. On a wall-plug basis the record shot's gain was about **0.02**. See [[q-gain-definitions]].

The enabling change for the 2025 record was **a higher-quality high-density-carbon (diamond) capsule** — not the laser. See [[rayleigh-taylor-instability]].


**As public infrastructure.** NIF represents about **$3.5 bn** of federal investment and is named,
alongside PPPL and DIII-D, as facility capacity that private fusion companies now depend on but
would not have built — the economics of a public good. See [[44-state-capital-and-the-public-balance-sheet]].
