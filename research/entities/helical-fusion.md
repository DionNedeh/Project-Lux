Tokyo developer drawing on Japan’s [[lhd|Large Helical Device]] lineage. Its design combines a helical [[stellarator]], HTS magnets and a liquid-metal blanket intended to serve both heat capture and the fuel cycle.

The [[fia-global-fusion-industry-report-2026]] records **$65 million declared funding**, 46 employees and an announced 50 MWe first pilot plant in the late 2030s. Those are company projections. Its reported component progress is more concrete: a **40 kA × 9 T HTS pancake-coil test**, operation of the GALOP liquid-metal blanket/divertor facility, and assembly of the Helix HARUKA intermediate machine beginning in March 2026. HARUKA is designed to integrate the company’s magnet and blanket and to demonstrate steady-state operation exceeding one day.

Helical Fusion also reports an offtake commitment from Japanese retailer AOKI SUPER. The commercial agreement does not demonstrate plant performance, but it does make the company one of the few stellarator developers pairing [[rebco]] engineering with an identified customer before a pilot device exists.
