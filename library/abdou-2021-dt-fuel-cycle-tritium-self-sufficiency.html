The canonical quantitative treatment of whether a D-T fusion plant can fuel itself. A 51-page Nuclear Fusion review from nine authors across UCLA, KIT, the ITER Organization, ORNL, INL and NFRI Korea, built on a time-dependent fuel-cycle dynamics model covering all components of the cycle.

Until this ingest the vault asserted the 1.05–1.15 design band on the authority of the DOE roadmap alone. This is the primary source that band comes from, with its reasoning attached.

Warning

The paper's primary conclusion is negative The authors state plainly that the physics and technology state-of-the-art will not enable DEMO and future power plants to satisfy the fuel-cycle requirements they set out. This is a review that concludes a gap, not a solution.

The central reframing: TBR is not the binding constraint#

The vault has treated tritium as a breeding problem. This paper's case is that breeding is downstream of two plasma parameters and one chemical-plant parameter.

What binds is the product of fuelling efficiency and burn fraction, η_f · f_b, together with the inner-cycle processing time t_p:

Condition Required TBR Start-up inventory (3 GW)
ITER's estimated f_b = 0.36%, 100% availability 1.82 >200 kg — the paper calls this unacceptable
η_f · f_b = 1% 1.08 —
η_f · f_b = 2%, t_p = 4 h — 11 kg
η_f · f_b = 5%, t_p = 1 h 1.02 <5 kg

Stated requirement: η_f · f_b > 2% and t_p of 1–4 h for self-sufficiency with reasonable confidence. Start-up inventory scales linearly with fusion power — a 100 MW facility needs about 1 kg.

See Tritium Burn Fraction and Tritium Start-up Inventory.

Note

Independently corroborated, 2026-10-19 ST-E1's fuel-cycle paper reports extraction sensitivity and fuelling efficiency as the dominant drivers of inventory performance, rather than marginal improvements in breeding ratio — this paper's central reframing, reached six years later by a company design team using inventory modelling of a liquid-lithium blanket, with no shared authorship. Read from an abstract only, and carrying no figures, so it corroborates the ordering of the parameters and not their values.

The achievability bands#

The paper's own confidence framing, which the vault now adopts with attribution:

  • Unlikely if required TBR > 1.15
  • Possible if 1.05 < TBR ≤ 1.15
  • Attained with high confidence if TBR ≤ 1.05

Against this, the maximum achievable TBR for the most detailed blanket system designs available is estimated at 1.15, with high confidence only at 1.05. The achievable range is given as 1.05–1.15 — so the requirement and the capability occupy the same interval, and the working margin is 10%.

Warning

A measured reason to distrust calculated TBR A series of integral blanket-neutronics experiments found calculation overestimated the tritium production rate by an average factor of 1.14, against a 5% measurement uncertainty. The authors call the overestimation "alarming" and a safety factor of >1.1 to 1.2 on calculated TBR was recommended. This is a sharper, sourced statement of the warning already on Tritium Breeding Ratio (TBR).

Availability is a tritium parameter#

The finding the authors themselves flag as significant, and the one with the widest consequences for this vault's coverage of near-term devices:

Availability factor Self-sufficiency
AF < 10% Impossible for any η_f · f_b
AF > 30%, 1% ≤ η_f · f_b ≤ 2% Possible
AF > 50%, η_f · f_b > 2% Achievable with reasonable confidence

This bears directly on the near-term facilities the vault treats as the path to a blanket demonstration — FNSF, VNS and CTF are all predicted below 30%. See Availability and RAMI.

Reserve inventory. A reserve time of 24 h leads to unacceptable reserve and start-up inventories. The conclusion is that reserves must cover only a few hours, which converts the problem into one of reliability and fast maintainability rather than storage.

Direct internal recycling#

The paper credits the EU-DEMO direct internal recycling concept — routing plasma exhaust straight to fuelling without passing the isotope separation system — with reducing overall processing time and inventories, and with a positive effect on required TBR. At η_f · f_b = 1%, recycling 80% of exhaust directly lowers required TBR from 1.120 to 1.075. See Direct Internal Recycling (DIR).

R&D gaps the paper names#

  • Cutting processing time, including batch to continuous tritium technology
  • Raising η_f · f_b toward 5%; high-field-side pellet injection is the favoured route, but sustained high-speed injection has been demonstrated only at 1 Hz, short-pulse capability
  • Separate D and T transport in core and pedestal, and the inward DT pinch
  • A fusion nuclear science facility sized to accumulate surplus tritium for DEMO start-up

Access limitations#

Inspected in full. The 51-page published version was captured to raw/ from IOP and extracted to raw/extracts/; all figures recorded above were read from that extract, not from a secondary summary. Bibliographic record verified against the article's own first page: Nucl. Fusion 61 (2021) 013001, DOI 10.1088/1741-4326/abbf35, © 2020 IAEA Vienna.

Licence not confirmed. IOP labels the article a "Free article" and serves the PDF without paywall, but states no open-access licence, and the PDF carries a per-article price line. Recorded here as free to read at the publisher, not as CC-licensed.

Note

Two bibliographic traps in this record One paper, not two. Web search results in this session reported "Abdou 2021" and "Nucl. Fusion 61 013001" as different papers — a 2021 review and a separate 2020 time-dependent model. The article's own first page settles it: they are the same paper, published online 23 November 2020 in the January 2021 issue. The source_date above is the online date.

A 1986 predecessor exists. Abdou et al., Deuterium–Tritium Fuel Self-Sufficiency in Fusion Reactors (1986), reached a compatible conclusion about restricted parameter space. It is not held in raw/ and nothing on this page derives from it.

What this source does not establish#

  • No measured plant fuel cycle. Every figure is model output or a design requirement. No D-T plant has ever closed its fuel cycle; this paper does not change that.
  • The loss assumption is weak by the authors' own account. Environmental tritium losses are set to 10⁻⁴ — and the paper says arbitrarily. Five components, including the blanket and plasma-facing components, are assigned zero direct environmental loss on the grounds that they sit inside the vacuum vessel. Published critiques of this paper attack the loss assumption; a figure of 0.1% circulates in that commentary and is not the paper's value, which is ten times smaller. The vault records the paper's own figure and does not endorse the critique.
  • Burning-plasma predictions are self-declared unreliable. The authors state that the maximum achievable burn fraction in ITER "remain[s] uncertain", that ITER fuelling estimates "cannot be fully validated" for lack of burning-plasma experiments, and that pellet fuelling efficiency at shallow penetration is "highly uncertain" and possibly optimistic.
  • Schedules in it are stale. The authors note that ITER and FNSF start-date results were calculated years earlier and the schedules have since slipped. Anything date-dependent here is read as of 2020, not 2026.
