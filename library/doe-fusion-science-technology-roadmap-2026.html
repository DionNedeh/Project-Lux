The US national fusion strategy. Part I October 2025; Part II June 2026. Input from over 800 scientists and engineers, 15+ private companies, 10+ national laboratories, 70+ universities.

Quote — Darío Gil, Under Secretary for Science: "a comprehensive national strategy to accelerate the development and commercialization of fusion energy by the mid-2030s."

Structure#

Build – Innovate – Grow.

Six Core Challenge Areas: A structural materials · B plasma-facing components and PMI · C confinement approaches · D fuel cycle and tritium · E blanket science · F plant engineering and system integration.

Eight Infrastructure Streams: plasma confinement · enabling technologies · exhaust and high-heat-flux · nuclear-effects testing · remote maintenance and balance-of-plant · blanket development · fuel-cycle development · HPC, advanced manufacturing and AI.

Quantified targets extracted#

  • "Milestone 1: Q > 1, first demonstration in a U.S. fusion device" — i.e. SPARC
  • Tritium Breeding Ratio (TBR) > 1.1 at FPP scale; long-term TRL 7 integrated blanket at TBR > 1.1
  • Irradiation datasets to ~10 dpa for Fe- and V-alloys — mid-term
  • Domestic industrial heats of multi-tonne Fe-alloy and 500 kg V-alloy
  • SPARC to demonstrate Q > 1 and progress toward Q > 5 with alpha-dominated plasmas
  • Conduct REBCO High-Temperature Superconductor irradiation campaigns to define magnet performance and standards
  • Codify fusion-specific requirements into ASME-based standards using validated small-specimen methods

The infrastructure sequencing#

Near term (2–3 yr) neutron-source surrogates and non-nuclear blanket test stands → mid term (3–5 yr) construct Fusion Prototypic Neutron Source (FPNS) → long term (5–10 yr) integrate with private pilot plants.
