Richmond, British Columbia. Magnetized Target Fusion: inject a magnetised plasma into a rotating cavity of liquid lead-lithium, then collapse the cavity mechanically.

The liquid metal is simultaneously first wall, breeding blanket and coolant — and being liquid, it cannot be damaged by neutrons. That is the architectural bet, and it sidesteps the field's hardest problem by construction.

The first publicly listed fusion company#

Completed its business combination with Spring Valley Acquisition Corp. III on 10 July 2026; trades on Nasdaq as GFUZ.
Implied pro-forma equity value ~US$1 bn
Committed, oversubscribed PIPE US$107.7 m
Trust capital (assuming no redemptions) US$230 m
Actually raised US$127 m

The discrepancy is the story. Institutional PIPE investors committed and were oversubscribed; the broader public market largely redeemed. Fusion's first genuine public-market price signal, and a cautious one. See The Investment Mechanics.

Lawson Machine 26 (LM26) has reached 0.72 keV and is funded through end-2028 against three published milestones.

The 2025 near-failure. In May 2025 General Fusion laid off at least 25% of staff while short of cash — days after Lawson Machine 26 (LM26) successfully compressed plasma. It had raised $440 m; its previous round was $22.66 m, closed July 2024. The SPAC merger fourteen months later makes this a near-failure and recovery, not a death. Source: Layoffs Hit General Fusion as the Fusion Power Startup Runs Short on Cash; analysed in Survivorship: Failure, Retrenchment and the Shape of the Industry.
