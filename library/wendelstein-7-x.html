The world's leading Stellarator, at Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics Greifswald. Quasi-isodynamically optimised — the vindication of forty years of stellarator optimisation theory.

Its 50 non-planar superconducting coils are shaped to roughly 1 mm tolerance and were, when built, among the most complex engineered objects in existence.

The records (2025 campaign, ended 22 May)#

  • World record Triple Product for plasma durations above 10 seconds, sustained across a 43-second discharge
  • Beats JET — Joint European Torus's long-pulse record with a plasma three times smaller and five times less heating power
  • Peak triple product held stable 1.9 s
  • Tokamak-level performance with stable peaked density profiles (Phys. Rev. E, 2026)

That a stellarator now leads a tokamak on any confinement figure of merit is a genuine reversal of the 1969 verdict — and it is the single most important reason the private stellarator sector exists.

The record discharge was fed by ~90 frozen hydrogen pellets from a one-of-a-kind injector built at Oak Ridge National Laboratory — see Pellet Injection.

What it has not done#

No tritium, no fusion power, and not designed for either. It is a confinement-physics machine, and it has largely done its job.

Its construction took over a decade and met stress corrosion cracking in cooling lines, coil case machining accuracy problems, and insulation failures at high-voltage test — see Failure Case Studies.
