A toroidal plasma with a pressure gradient spontaneously drives its own toroidal current, via trapped-particle dynamics. Free current, and the only credible route to a steady-state Tokamak power plant.

Why it matters#

A tokamak's plasma current is normally driven inductively by a central solenoid acting as a transformer — and a transformer has a finite volt-second budget. When it runs out, the pulse ends. The alternatives are:

  1. Accept pulsed operation, with thermal storage (ITER, EU DEMO baseline) — see Grid Integration and Plant Operations
  2. Drive current non-inductively with beams or RF, at a Recirculating Power cost
  3. Bootstrap fraction of 70–90%, driving only the remainder externally

Option 3 is the advanced-tokamak scenario, and the bootstrap fraction rises with poloidal beta.

The catch#

High bootstrap fraction requires broad, hollow current profiles that are more MHD-unstable, reached through internal transport barriers that are themselves delicate. Steady-state is a genuinely harder physics problem than pulsed operation.

Stellarators avoid the question entirely — their confining field is external. In a stellarator bootstrap current is usually a nuisance to be minimised.
