$450 million raised in the year to July 2026 — one of the largest raises in the sector, for a company that makes targets and fuel rather than a reactor.

The August 2026 result#

With Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, Inertia cut cryogenic deuterium-tritium fuel-layer formation from multiple days to 2–3 hours, with a path to under an hour, while maintaining ignition-proven design tolerances. On 20 August it reported reducing fuel pellet filling time from days to minutes.

Why this matters more than it sounds#

A laser IFE plant firing at 15 Hz consumes roughly 500 million targets per year at a unit cost below $0.50. Target throughput is a first-order barrier — arguably the first-order barrier, since National Ignition Facility proved the physics and left manufacturing as the constraint. See Component Manufacturing.

Inertia also noted that a 10 MJ laser is less sensitive to fuel imperfections than NIF's 2 MJ system — meaning a bigger driver buys tolerance, which buys manufacturability. Secondary benefits: cheaper targets and lower tritium inventory, since less tritium sits in the fabrication pipeline at any moment.
