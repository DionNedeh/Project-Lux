A toroidal device in which the entire confining field, including the rotational transform, is generated by external coils. No plasma current is required.

Invented by Lyman Spitzer at Princeton in 1951 (Project Matterhorn). Abandoned worldwide within two years of the 1969 T-3 results. Now, in 2026, holding the world Triple Product record for pulses over 10 seconds, and outnumbering tokamaks among private fusion companies 8 to 5.

Why it is the better reactor concept#

  • No disruptions — the tokamak's principal failure mode simply does not exist
  • Intrinsically steady-state — no transformer, no dwell, no thermal cycling, no storage system. See Grid Integration and Plant Operations
  • No Greenwald Density Limit of the tokamak type

Why it is the worse construction project#

Wendelstein 7-X's 50 non-planar superconducting coils are shaped to roughly 1 mm tolerance. Manufacturing cost and schedule risk are severe, and maintenance access through a 3D coil set is genuinely difficult — the strongest practical argument against the concept.

Thea Energy attacks that constraint by replacing bespoke non-planar coils with many individually powered planar coils. Its Canis prototype demonstrated manufacture and closed-loop control of a flat nine-coil REBCO High-Temperature Superconductor array within 1% of predicted static field. It did not form closed flux surfaces or a plasma, so Eos remains an integrated-machine test rather than a proven reactor architecture. Evidence and limits: Prototyping and Test of the Canis HTS Planar Coil Array for Stellarator Field Shaping.

The architecture scaled to a plant is Helios, whose published preconceptual design quantifies two of the concept's standing claims: recirculating power near 15% of generated electricity rather than a tokamak's 20–40%, since an ignited stellarator needs under 1 MW of heating; and no thermal-storage system, because output never pulses. It also claims the first stellarator equilibrium explicitly designed around a tokamak-like X-point Divertor, addressing the unsolved exhaust problem in W7-X's island-divertor line. All of it is simulation — see Overview of the Helios Design: A Practical Planar Coil Stellarator Fusion Power Plant.

What changed#

Computation. An unoptimised 3D field has catastrophic neoclassical transport. Once fields could be numerically optimised for Quasi-symmetry and Quasi-isodynamicity or quasi-isodynamicity, the problem became tractable by design.
