policysafety

China’s Ministry of Ecology and Environment issued this notice on 2 April 2025 and published it on 7 April 2025. It replaces a 2016 instruction for magnetic-confinement devices and establishes a more explicit administrative framework for fusion radiation safety.

The notice separates devices into three categories: plasma-physics experimental devices, deuterium–tritium fusion experimental devices, and fusion-energy application devices. The Ministry supervises them by reference to the radiation-safety regime for nuclear-technology-use facilities and issues radiation-safety licences; regional nuclear and radiation safety offices conduct routine inspection. Environmental-impact assessment and radiation licensing are connected, with a combined review to be explored. Existing approvals remain valid.

This is an adopted administrative instrument, not a company roadmap. It demonstrates that China has differentiated fusion devices by experimental and fuel/application characteristics while retaining central radiation oversight. It does not by itself specify every future commercial design standard, liability rule, security obligation or decommissioning requirement.

The framework differs from both the United Kingdom’s explicit exclusion from nuclear-site licensing and the United States’ byproduct-material pathway. Document 41 uses the notice to compare institutional architectures without treating any one jurisdiction’s lower-burden language as evidence of lower physical risk.
