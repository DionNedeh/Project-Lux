An IAEA-convened review, open access under CC-BY 4.0, covering waste arising from ITER, demonstration machines and power plants. Text extracted to raw/extracts/gonzalez-de-vicente-2022-fusion-waste.txt (13,848 words). It is the corpus's first primary source on how fusion waste is classified and where it goes, and it is the evidential backbone of Fusion Waste: Classification, Clearance and Disposal.

What it establishes#

Fusion waste is LLW and ILW, with no transuranics. The authors state that fusion "should be able to avoid creating high-level waste". The activation inventory is dominated by tritium and activation products rather than actinides, and decay heat and long-term radiotoxicity are correspondingly lower than fission.

The volume, however, is larger. EU DEMO could generate as much as 10,000 t of solid waste from in-vessel components alone, against roughly 2,000 t from the European sodium-cooled fast reactor plus about 300 t of spent fuel across its lifetime — 2,500 t of ILW and HLW in total. The paper is explicit that the burden is significant "both in terms of pure volume and volume per unit of electricity produced". This is the single most important number in the source, because it contradicts the common public framing that fusion has solved the waste problem. It has not; it has changed its class while increasing its volume.

Impurities, not fuel, set the disposal route. The dominant wastes are structural steels — EUROFER97, F82H, AISI 316L, bainitic steels, JK2LB (see Reduced-Activation Steels). The long-lived isotopes that cause trouble come from alloying elements (niobium, molybdenum, nickel, carbon, nitrogen, copper, aluminium) and from uncontrolled impurities of those elements plus potassium and cobalt. "After irradiation, these isotopes might preclude disposal in LLW repositories."

Classification is jurisdictional. An alpha limit of 4 MBq/kg is cited for France's LILW-SL class, and ⁹⁴Nb activity may exceed France's nuclide-specific limit while satisfying UK repository criteria. The same physical object can be two different waste classes in two countries.

Recycling and clearance are load-bearing. The authors describe efforts to recycle and clear as "essential to support fusion deployment", not as an optimisation.

Limits#

Design-stage assessment throughout: the tonnages are for conceptual plants, not measured inventories, and no fusion power plant has yet been decommissioned. The paper predates the 2024–26 regulatory developments covered in Fusion Regulation Beyond the United States.
