A multiscale tritium-transport study of ST-E1, Tokamak Energy's preliminary fusion pilot plant, coupling component-level 1D models of plasma-facing components to a 17-subsystem fuel-cycle model inside TMAP8, with Gaussian-process surrogates standing in for the expensive component simulations. Authors are from INL and Tokamak Energy; the plant design sits under the DOE milestone programme.

Warning

This is an unreviewed preprint, and its confidence is set accordingly arXiv v1, submitted to Nuclear Fusion, not peer-reviewed. It also contains internal numerical inconsistencies visible in the paper itself (recorded below). It is ingested because it is the only ST-E1 fuel-cycle modelling in reach, and a labelled weak source is worth more than a silent gap — but nothing in this vault should rest on it alone.

What it is, and is not, about#

It computes no TBR and no self-sufficiency result. The breeding ratio is an input. What the paper produces is tritium retention and recovery behaviour in components, and the effect of feeding surrogate-derived residence times into a system-level cycle model.

The paper also notes ST-E1 is not intended to supply tritium to further plants, which removes the doubling-time pressure that drives much of Abdou's analysis.

The declared inputs#

From Table 6, attributed to Tokamak Energy — all of these are inputs, none are findings:

Parameter Value
Tritium breeding ratio 1.113
Tritium burn efficiency (TBE) 0.02
Availability factor 0.375
Tritium burn rate 2.7938 × 10⁻⁶ kg/s
Tritium decay rate 1.78 × 10⁻⁹ s⁻¹
Non-radioactive losses ε_i per-component, 1.62 × 10⁻⁸ to 3.33 × 10⁻⁷; zero for all others

The input TBR of 1.113 sits inside Abdou's possible band (1.05–1.15), not the high-confidence band.

Warning

TBE is tabulated but never defined The paper gives TBE = 0.02 in Table 6 and defines the term nowhere in its text. Whether this denotes Abdou's product η_f · f_b or the burn fraction f_b alone cannot be determined from this source. The difference decides whether ST-E1's assumption sits exactly on Abdou's 2% floor or at roughly a quarter of it. The vault records the ambiguity rather than the favourable reading. This is the unqualified-symbol error class in a new place.

Vault derivation, not a paper figure: the stated burn rate over a year at the stated availability gives roughly 33 kg of tritium per year (88 kg/yr at continuous duty × 0.375). Recorded as arithmetic on the paper's inputs, for scale against the 30–40 kg world inventory on Tritium Breeding Ratio (TBR).

Components and materials#

Component Armour Pipe
Divertor 3 or 8 mm W 1 mm V44 or 1 mm W
Centre-column first wall 5 mm W 5 mm V44
Blanket first wall 5 mm W 2.5 or 5 mm V44
Vacuum vessel none 50 mm stainless steel

V44 is V-4Cr-4Ti, a vanadium alloy — notable because the vault's structural-materials coverage is RAFM steel and tungsten. Only the divertor and the two first-wall components use surrogate-derived residence times; the remaining 14 subsystems use baseline values.

Results#

  • Component inventories saturate after roughly 10,000 s, reaching quasi-steady state before the modelled 50 pulses complete (50 pulses × 600 s injection + 1,000 s dwell, 80,000 s total).
  • Thinner armour lowers retention but raises coolant-side losses — a design trade the paper surfaces without resolving.
  • Bake-out works, on these assumptions. Heating the 8 mm W armour / 1 mm W pipe divertor case to 873 K releases over 95% of retained tritium in about 33 h (30 h ramp, 170 h hold), with chamber pressure reaching a minimum of 1.93 × 10⁻³ Pa.
  • Surrogate accuracy is strongly asymmetric. Steady-state flux is predicted well — best RMSPE 1.60% for the W-pipe case, 9.92% for V44. Residence times are predicted poorly: 8.35% / 13.68% (W pipe) and 23.90% / 31.26% (V44) for τ₀ and τ₁. Residence time is precisely the quantity fed into the system model.

Internal inconsistencies preserved#

Three, all visible within the paper:

  1. Cost reduction. §3.1 claims an "order-of-magnitude" lower computational cost; the discussion states a factor of 2.7 × 10⁻⁶. These differ by five orders of magnitude and the paper does not reconcile them. No wall-clock timings are given.
  2. Residence-time uncertainty. The discussion cites 33.58% and 50.57%; the results section gives 23.90% and 31.26% for the same quantities.
  3. Bake-out temperatures. Table 1 lists 873 K for the V44-pipe divertor and 673 K for the W-pipe divertor; the body text gives the reverse. The authors flag this one themselves.

Additionally, up to 20% of training simulations were discarded for numerical instability or for fits exceeding the 80,000 s window — a selection effect on the surrogate training set that the paper notes but does not quantify the consequences of.

A first attempt to read the PDF over the web returned compressed binary and recovered nothing — the local extraction is the path that worked, and the earlier failure is recorded here so it is not mistaken for absence.

Warning

Updated 2026-10-19 — the authoritative design paper is now held, and it does not match Willis et al. 2026 was captured after this page was written. Two of this preprint's inputs disagree with it:

Quantity This preprint Willis et al.
TBR 1.113 > 1.14 (RDP table); ~1.1 (prose)
Availability factor 0.375 > 80% (mission target)

Neither disagreement is explained by any source. The availability gap matters because 37.5% and 80% fall either side of Abdou's 50% self-sufficiency threshold, so the two papers imply different conclusions about the same plant. A plausible reconciliation is that this preprint models an early or pilot-phase operating point — but no source states that, and the vault does not assume it. Recorded under Known conflicts in Research library.

What this source does not establish#

  • Nothing about whether ST-E1 achieves tritium self-sufficiency. TBR is an input; the paper frames low-inventory self-sufficient operation as future work.
  • Nothing measured. No ST-E1 hardware exists. This is simulation of a preliminary design, using literature material properties with no uncertainty quantification.
  • Retention is deliberately overestimated. The authors state peak tritium fluxes are conservative and likely too high because re-emission is excluded, coolant-side tritium concentration and surface partial pressure are both fixed at zero, and geometry is 1D only.
  • Trapping physics is simplified. A single intrinsic trap per material is modelled where real materials have several; irradiation damage, dynamic trapping, isotopic competition and mixed D–T surface effects are all excluded. Irradiation damage is the omission that matters most for a neutron-loaded first wall.
