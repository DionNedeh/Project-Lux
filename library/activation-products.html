What makes a fusion plant radioactive. Fusion produces no fission products and no actinides; the 14.1 MeV neutron flux instead transmutes the atoms of the surrounding structure, turning the machine itself into the radioactive inventory.

This single fact shapes fusion's safety case, its waste classification and — unexpectedly — its procurement specifications.

Why it is a materials-selection problem#

Activation is elemental, not structural. Which isotopes appear depends on which elements are present, so the mitigation is to build alloys from elements whose activation products are short-lived: replace molybdenum with tungsten, niobium with tantalum, and eliminate nickel, cobalt and copper wherever possible. That principle is what reduced-activation ferritic- martensitic steels exist to satisfy.

Use conventional stainless steel, with its nickel, cobalt and niobium content, and long-lived activation products appear that will not decay to a disposable level on any useful timescale.

Impurities, not the specification#

The awkward finding in Overview on the Management of Radioactive Waste from Fusion Facilities is that the troublesome isotopes frequently come from elements nobody specified. ⁹⁴Nb is produced from niobium present at parts-per-million as a tramp element in commercial steel; potassium and cobalt arrive the same way. After irradiation these "might preclude disposal in LLW repositories".

Activation therefore turns into a supply-chain requirement — dedicated melts and verified feedstock across thousands of tonnes — as set out in Fusion Waste: Classification, Clearance and Disposal and Critical Materials and Supply Chains.

Why it is still better than fission#

Activation products are overwhelmingly short-lived relative to actinides, and there are no transuranics. Radiotoxicity falls over decades rather than geological time, and the associated Decay Heat is low enough that passive cooling suffices — which is why the accident pathways in Safety, Environment and Decommissioning stay bounded in a way fission's do not.
