The Soviet Tokamak that converted the world.

At the August 1968 IAEA conference in Novosibirsk, the Soviet delegation reported T-3 had achieved electron temperatures of about 1 keV and confinement an order of magnitude better than any Western device. The claim was received with considerable scepticism — memories of ZETA were fresh.

The Culham expedition#

In a remarkable episode of Cold War scientific cooperation, the Soviets invited a British team from Culham to bring their newly developed laser Thomson scattering diagnostic to Moscow and measure T-3 themselves. Led by Nicol Peacock and Derek Robinson, they did — and confirmed the result in 1969.

The effect was immediate and total. Within about two years Princeton had converted its Model C stellarator into a tokamak, and essentially every major fusion laboratory in the world reoriented. Historians call it the great tokamak stampede. The Stellarator would spend the next forty years in the wilderness.
