policyroadmap

Published on 16 March 2026, the UK Fusion Strategy places regulation, industrial capability, research infrastructure and international partnership inside one delivery programme. For regulation, it confirms the policy implemented through the Energy Act 2023: fusion facilities are not licensed as nuclear installations under the fission-oriented nuclear-site regime.

Instead, the Environment Agency and Health and Safety Executive—or devolved equivalents—remain the principal environmental and worker-safety regulators. The strategy presents this as proportionate, technology-appropriate regulation, while also acknowledging unfinished work. Security and other residual regulatory positions are to be clarified during the current Parliament.

The adopted fact is therefore the institutional route and nuclear-site-licensing exclusion. The complete future framework is partly designed or announced, not yet wholly implemented. The strategy’s delivery ambitions, including support for STEP, are government commitments and targets rather than demonstrated commercial outcomes.

For comparison in Document 41, the UK model is function-based: existing environmental, occupational and radiological institutions regulate the hazards unless evidence justifies a fusion-specific addition. That differs institutionally from China’s classified radiation-licensing notice and from jurisdictions still determining whether fusion belongs inside a nuclear-installation framework. The document does not establish that regulation alone will determine deployment speed; planning, grid connection, tritium, waste, safeguards and public consent remain separate constraints.
