The Zero Energy Thermonuclear Assembly at Harwell — when built, the largest and most powerful fusion device in the world, and the source of fusion's most instructive public failure.

On 24 January 1958 the UKAEA announced ZETA had produced neutrons of thermonuclear origin, with press coverage suggesting Britain had beaten the world to fusion power.

By May 1958 the team had established the neutrons came from beam-target reactions driven by plasma instabilities accelerating ions — not from a thermal plasma at fusion temperature. The retraction was public and painful.

Why it keeps mattering#

Neutron count is easy to measure; temperature is hard. A non-thermal ion population produces neutrons at a rate that looks impressive and scales nowhere. This is why the Triple Product, which requires an independent confinement-time measurement, is the metric that matters — and why any modern claim reporting temperature or yield without a confinement time should be read carefully. See The Discourse.

ZETA's Triple Product was 9.0 × 10¹⁴ — the field's baseline. SPARC's projection is roughly seven million times higher.

The good consequence#

The fiasco made clear that fusion claims could not be verified in secrecy. It led directly to comprehensive declassification at the second Geneva conference in September 1958.
