The design paper that reorganised the field. Published from MIT in 2015, it converted the high-field compact tokamak from an argument into a point design.

R = 3.3 m, B₀ = 9.2 T, I_p ≈ 7.8 MA, 525 MW fusion, ~200 MWe.

The three architectural choices#

All three are about maintenance and lifetime, not plasma physics — which is what makes the paper unusual:

  1. Demountable toroidal field magnets — the entire vacuum vessel can be removed and replaced as a unit, making the most-irradiated component a consumable
  2. FLiBe liquid immersion blanket — moderator, breeder, coolant and shield simultaneously
  3. REBCO High-Temperature Superconductor HTS — the enabling technology, via The B⁴ Scaling

The design philosophy#

ARC enforces significant margin to disruptive operational limits: strict limits on edge Safety Factor q, Greenwald Density Limit fraction, and normalised beta below the no-wall limit.

A genuinely different bet from the ARIES advanced-tokamak line, which sought performance by operating near limits with active stabilisation. ARC buys margin with magnetic field instead of with control systems.

Realised commercially as ARC at Chesterfield County, Virginia.
