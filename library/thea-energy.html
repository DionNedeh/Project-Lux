companies

Kearny, New Jersey. Formerly Princeton Stellarators.

The distinctive idea: rather than complex 3D coils, Thea uses arrays of simple planar coils whose individually controlled currents synthesise the required 3D field. Canis demonstrated the component-level premise: nine planar REBCO High-Temperature Superconductor coils operated at 20 K and controlled two static field patterns with 0.56% and 0.60% RMS error. That is not yet an integrated stellarator result; Eos remains planned.

2026#

  • First US Department of Energy — Office of Fusion Milestone Program awardee to complete a full fusion pilot plant design review — a genuine sector first. See Milestone Tranching
  • $20 m ARPA-E SCALEUP award (July 2026) to establish the first US production line for modular REBCO High-Temperature Superconductor HTS magnets
  • Series B extension (August 2026) on top of a $100 m round led by Thomas Tull's US Innovative Technology Fund
  • Stellarator digital twin partnership with NVIDIA, Synopsys, Argonne and Princeton — notable for including an EDA company, signalling a chip-industry approach to design verification. See Simulation, Codes and Nuclear Data

Machines: Canis (magnet-array prototype, built) → Eos (planned demonstrator) → Helios (planned power plant). Evidence and limits: Prototyping and Test of the Canis HTS Planar Coil Array for Stellarator Field Shaping for the hardware, Overview of the Helios Design: A Practical Planar Coil Stellarator Fusion Power Plant for the plant design.

The design review noted above now has a published output: a Fusion Engineering and Design special issue on Helios, whose overview paper sets a 1,094 MW thermal, ~390 MWe net design point with a full balance-of-plant accounting. It remains a preconceptual design — Thea's only hardware result is still Canis.
