The US public fusion programme. Fusion Energy Sciences received roughly $790–806 million in FY2026 — less than one-third of the capitalisation of the single state-owned company China created for fusion in July 2025.

The Fusion Science and Technology Roadmap#

Part I October 2025, Part II June 2026. Developed with input from over 800 scientists and engineers, 15+ private companies, 10+ national laboratories and 70+ universities. Targets commercialisation by the mid-2030s.

Strategy: Build – Innovate – Grow.

Six Core Challenge Areas: A structural materials · B plasma-facing components and PMI · C advancing confinement approaches · D fuel cycle and tritium processing · E blanket science · F plant engineering and system integration.

Eight Infrastructure Streams, including nuclear-effects testing, blanket development, fuel-cycle development, and HPC/AI.

Named headline objective: "Milestone 1: Q > 1, first demonstration in a U.S. fusion device" — which in practice means SPARC.

Programmes#

  • Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program — eight companies, NASA-COTS-inspired. See Milestone Tranching
  • INFUSE — connects companies to national lab capability
  • IFE-STAR — $42 m across three inertial fusion hubs
  • FIRE Collaboratives — $107 m
  • Genesis Mission — AI/HPC initiative, first projects selected July 2026

The roadmap disclaims funding commitments on nearly every page: "This Roadmap does not commit the Department of Energy to specific funding levels."

As an investor. DOE Fusion Energy Sciences ran to $790 m in 2025 and $806 m in 2026, with roughly 1.2% of 2020–2023 funding directed to commercialisation — it remains substantially a science programme. DOE has newly established an Office of Fusion. See State Capital and the Public Balance Sheet.
