The narrow edge region of steep pressure gradient created by H-mode. Because core profiles are roughly stiff — their gradients pinned near a turbulence threshold — the pedestal height sets the whole plasma's pressure, and therefore its fusion power.

A 20% improvement in pedestal pressure is worth roughly 40% in fusion power. This is why pedestal physics receives attention disproportionate to the volume it occupies.

EPED#

The EPED model predicts pedestal height and width from two constraints acting together:

  • Peeling-ballooning MHD stability — the limit at which edge current and pressure gradient drive a large-scale instability. Reaching it is an Edge-Localised Mode (ELM)
  • Kinetic ballooning mode — a local turbulence constraint setting the pedestal width

EPED is one of the few genuinely predictive models in tokamak physics.

Super H-mode#

EPED predicts a bifurcation: in strongly shaped plasmas above a critical pedestal density there is a second, much higher stable branch. Accessed experimentally on DIII-D.

If sustainable at reactor scale it is a large free performance gain. Whether it is compatible with a detached divertor and reactor densities is unresolved — the recurring core-edge tension.
