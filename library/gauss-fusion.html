Garching-based European consortium developing a deuterium–tritium Stellarator power plant. The The Global Fusion Industry Report 2026 records $31.53 million declared funding, 35 employees and a stated 1,000 MWe commercial design.

Gauss treats the reactor as an integrated engineering programme: superconducting magnets, tritium breeding and the closed fuel cycle, advanced materials, and remote handling. In the year to July 2026 it reported a conceptual design, a patent for demountable coils and the start of a tritium-breeding-blanket and fuel-cycle project.

Its dates are company projections, not demonstrated results: Q_sci > 1 in 2041, engineering net energy in 2045 and a first pilot plant in 2045. The report also says its component validation depends on external neutron infrastructure including IFMIF-DONES and proposed compact accelerator neutron sources. That dependency puts the same materials bottleneck identified in Engineering Challenges directly on its critical path.
