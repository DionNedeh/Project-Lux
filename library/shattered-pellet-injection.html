ITER's disruption mitigation system, first demonstrated at DIII-D in 2010.

Method. Fire large cryogenic pellets — for ITER a neon-hydrogen mixture — down a guide tube ending in a bend plate. The pellet shatters into a fragment spray before entering the plasma. Fragments ablate rapidly and distribute material far more effectively than an intact pellet, radiating thermal energy isotropically (protecting the divertor from a localised dump) and raising density enormously to increase collisional drag on incipient Runaway Electrons.

Two-stage injection#

Modelling shows a staggered scheme — pure hydrogen first, then a mixed hydrogen-neon pellet — substantially improves fragment assimilation and suppresses the hot-tail runaway mechanism, in which the fastest electrons fail to cool during a rapid thermal quench and become the seed.

The limitation#

Staggered injection outperforms single-stage, including with radioactive runaway sources — but see Runaway Electrons: no acceptable outcome has yet been found for a full-current D-T Disruption.
