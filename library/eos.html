Thea Energy's planned integrated deuterium-deuterium planar-coil Stellarator and neutron-source demonstrator, intended to precede the larger deuterium-tritium Helios power-plant concept.

Instead of winding a small number of bespoke three-dimensional modular coils, Eos is designed to use arrays of individually powered planar coils to synthesise the required three-dimensional magnetic field. The manufacturing claim has partial hardware support: Canis, a flat nine-coil REBCO High-Temperature Superconductor array, was manufactured and operated at 20 K and controlled two static field patterns within 1% of prediction.

The preconceptual design of the plant Eos is meant to precede is now published (Overview of the Helios Design: A Practical Planar Coil Stellarator Fusion Power Plant), which fixes what Eos must eventually retire: a two-field-period quasi-axisymmetric equilibrium with 324 individually controlled shaping coils, and a tokamak-like X-point Divertor never yet built in a stellarator.

What remains designed rather than demonstrated is the integrated machine. Canis produced no toroidal flux, closed magnetic surfaces, quasisymmetry or plasma, and did not reproduce Eos-scale forces, currents or component density. Eos's plasma performance and neutron-source role therefore remain planned. See Prototyping and Test of the Canis HTS Planar Coil Array for Stellarator Field Shaping for the component result and its limits.
