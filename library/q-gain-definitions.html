Where most public confusion about fusion lives. Never write "Q" unqualified.

Symbol Definition Measures
Q_sci P_fusion / P_heating-into-plasma The plasma alone. Ignores everything else
Q_target Fusion yield / laser energy on target National Ignition Facility's headline number
Q_wp Fusion energy / total grid electricity drawn The honest engineering figure
Q_eng Net electricity exported / consumed Whether the plant is a generator or a load

The gap in practice#

NIF's 7 April 2025 shot: Q_target = 4.13 (8.6 MJ from 2.08 MJ on target). The laser draws roughly 300–400 MJ from the grid, so Q_wp ≈ 0.02. Both figures are correct; only one was widely reported.

JET — Joint European Torus's famous 1997 0.67 is a transient peak. The harmonised value for the same shot is 0.27. See Appendix: Quantitative Progress Data.

Landmarks#

  • Q = 1 — scientific breakeven
  • Q = 5 — alpha self-heating equals external heating
  • Q ≈ 10 — ITER's target; a genuine Burning Plasma
  • Q = ∞ — Ignition
  • Q_eng > 1 — typically needs Q_sci of 10–30+, because of Recirculating Power

The error is not only journalistic#

Added 2026-10-19. Peer-reviewed design papers do it too, which is why this page's rule applies to primary sources and not just to press coverage.

ST-E1 is the clearest recent case: the reference design point in Willis et al. 2026 tabulates "Q (–)" with no subscript — 20 for the pilot phase, 50–70 for the commercial phase — and the surrounding prose says only "fusion gain". Arithmetic on the paper's own rows resolves it: 1500 MW fusion over 75 MW auxiliary is exactly 20, so the quantity is Q_sci. That subscript is a vault derivation; the source never supplies it.

Why it matters here rather than being pedantry: a reader who took ST-E1's "Q = 20" for Q_eng or Q_wp would conclude the plant exports twenty times its consumption. The same paper's own figures show the pilot phase producing 300–500 MWe net from 1500 MW fusion — a different ratio entirely, and the one that decides whether the plant is a generator or a load.

A related case, in a different quantity: the ST-E1 fuel-cycle modelling tabulates a "tritium burn efficiency" of 0.02 and defines it nowhere, leaving it undecidable whether it means a burn fraction or a burn-fraction-times-fuelling-efficiency product. See Tritium Burn Fraction. The failure mode generalises beyond Q: a bare symbol in a table is a defect wherever a field uses one symbol for several quantities.
