The source that addresses the continuous-flow problem in tritium accountancy directly: conventional accountancy assumes static batches in controlled conditions, and a fusion plant will run continuously.

Note

Accepted manuscript, not the version of record The capture is the DOE accepted manuscript released through OSTI PAGES, not Elsevier's published version. Content was not compared against the version of record. Authored under contract 89303321CEM000080 for the DOE Office of Environmental Management; the paper's acknowledgements also credit support from the Office of Fusion Energy Sciences.

Method#

A simple fuel-cycle concept is defined and each component's tritium inventory evolved in time by Euler approximation of a series of differential equations in Python. Sensor measurements are then simulated at specific points and passed through a Kalman Filter — a state-prediction algorithm standard in radar, robotics and hydrology, which the authors state had not previously been applied to tritium inventory accountancy.

The result#

Stage Percent error against the model's true value
Simulated raw sensor 64.37%
Kalman Filter, end of day 1 ≈ 0.633%
Kalman Filter, end of day 3 ≈ 0.001%

The authors measure this against "the accountancy standard of 1%" — the filter crosses that threshold within a day of simulated operation. A tenfold increase in sampling frequency tightens the estimate further and reaches its optimum earlier.

The limitation the authors state themselves#

Warning

The filter fails on accumulating bias Given a measurement system with a bias accumulating over the three-day period, the Kalman Filter "accurately predicts the mean of the sensor measurement but has an increasingly large spread from the true value" — which is useless, because the true value is what accountancy needs. A Bias-Aware Kalman Filter is named as ongoing work, deferred to a follow-on paper.

This matters because the paper also names the conditions a real fuel-cycle sensor will face — high vibration, ground-loop issues, radiofrequency-generating components — all of which accumulate exactly the biases the implemented filter cannot handle.

What it does not establish#

  • No real sensor data. Every measurement is simulated from the model it is then validated against, so the 0.001% figure measures self-consistency, not instrument performance.
  • A simple fuel cycle. The authors note designs have grown more complex since Abdou's 1985 paper, and that understanding plasma-vessel contributions and losses is required before this is usable commercially — the same unmeasurable terms recorded in Tritium Accountancy.
  • Not the 1% standard's provenance. The paper cites the figure without deriving it.
  • Nishi et al. 2006 is this paper's reference [1] — "Study on tritium accountancy in fusion DEMO plant at JAERI", Fusion Engineering and Design 81(1-7) 745–751. That citation is now confirmed from a held source, but the paper itself remains not held, so the instrument accuracies on Tritium Accountancy still reach this vault at second hand.
