Affordable, Robust, Compact. The first grid-scale commercial fusion power plant, and the design that reorganised the field when published from MIT in 2015.

The 2015 paper#

R = 3.3 m, B₀ = 9.2 T, I_p ≈ 7.8 MA, 525 MW fusion, ~200 MWe. Three architectural choices define it — all three about maintenance and lifetime, not plasma physics:

  1. Demountable toroidal field magnets — joints allow the entire vacuum vessel to be removed and replaced as a unit. The most-irradiated component becomes a consumable
  2. FLiBe liquid immersion blanket — moderator, tritium breeder, coolant and shield simultaneously
  3. REBCO High-Temperature Superconductor HTS — the enabling technology

The design philosophy#

ARC enforces significant margin to disruptive operational limits — strict limits on edge Safety Factor q, Greenwald Density Limit fraction, and normalised beta kept below the no-wall limit. It buys margin with magnetic field rather than with control systems. A genuinely different bet from the ARIES advanced-tokamak line.

The commercial plant#

~400 MWe from ~1.1 GW fusion, at the James River Industrial Center, Chesterfield County, Virginia — the Fall Line Fusion Power Station, 94 acres. Cost >$2.5 bn (≈ $6,250/kW, the field's first real cost datum — see Fusion LCOE and Capital Cost).

  • Conditional-use permit approved unanimously
  • First fusion company to apply to PJM Interconnection
  • Google holds a 200 MW PPA — half the output — plus equity

The best-modelled fuel cycle in the corpus#

ARC-class is the reference case for two MIT-led fuel-cycle studies, which makes it the design whose tritium behaviour this vault can characterise in most depth — though none of it is CFS's own work.

Meschini et al. 2023 model a 2LiF + BeF₂ liquid immersion blanket and find required TBR stays below 1.2 if tritium burn efficiency reaches 0.5–1% — conditional on availability above 70%, processing under 4 h and DIR.

Meschini et al. 2025 then add trapping, and the picture worsens materially: first-wall and vacuum-vessel inventory up a factor of 10³–10⁴, doubling time +50%, start-up inventory +30%, required TBR +2–5%.

These are academic models of an ARC-class plant, not statements about the Chesterfield machine, and no measurement exists for any of it.
