The first source in this vault to apply Abdou et al.'s fuel-cycle method to named private-sector plant designs rather than to a generic DEMO. Full text held; CC BY 4.0, version of record.

Note

Citation year This paper cites its parent as Abdou et al 2020 Nucl. Fusion 61 013001. That is the same paper this vault holds as Physics and Technology Considerations for the Deuterium–Tritium Fuel Cycle and Conditions for Tritium Fuel Self Sufficiency — online 2020, issue-dated 2021. One work, two years in circulation.

What it models#

A time-dependent, system-level fuel cycle in Matlab Simulink, applied to three designs: ARC-class, breeding in a 2LiF + BeF₂ (FLiBe) liquid immersion blanket; and two STEP-class variants, one with a liquid-lithium blanket, one with an encapsulated breeding blanket of FLiBe pebbles, gas-cooled.

The headline requirement#

Required TBR stays below 1.2 — but only if tritium burn efficiency reaches 0.5–1%. The paper calls that "significant, but attainable" improvement over current ability. It is bounded against a maximum achievable TBR the authors put at 1.1–1.2 depending on blanket design, so the margin is thin by construction.

Three further conditions come attached, and all three are demanding:

Requirement Value
Plant availability > 70%
Tritium processing time < 4 h
Direct internal recycling required

Why burn fraction dominates, in one number#

Run the model at ITER's expected achievable burn fraction of f_b = 0.36% — two pellet injectors, maximum fuelling 120 Pa m³ s⁻¹ — and the required TBR for self-sufficiency in DEMO becomes 1.82, which the authors note is far above anything achievable. This is the paper's clearest statement of the reframing η_f · f_b carries: breeding cannot rescue a plant that burns almost none of what it is fed.

Doubling time buys relief. At t_d = 1 year, self-sufficiency is "highly unlikely" below 1% burn efficiency; at t_d = 5 years it becomes "much easier to achieve".

What it does not establish#

  • Nothing measured. A system-level model of two unbuilt plant classes, benchmarked against no operating fuel cycle, because none exists.
  • Not ST-E1. The STEP-class liquid-lithium case is the nearest analogue to ST-E1's architecture, but it is STEP, modelled independently of Tokamak Energy.
  • No trapping. Tritium held in first wall, divertor and blanket is outside this model — the gap its own authors closed two years later in Impact of trapping on tritium self-sufficiency and tritium inventories in fusion power plant fuel cycles.
