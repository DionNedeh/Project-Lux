The only live instrument-development programme on tritium accountancy that this vault holds. A conference programme entry from the UKAEA Tritium Fuel Cycle division, working with LIBRTI, describing two named projects. It answers the question the American sources leave open — who is actually building the missing measurements.

Two projects, two halves of the problem#

HERA — real-time analysis of gas streams. Tests a mass spectrometer and a Raman spectrometer, aiming to quantify H₂, D₂ and T₂ from the limit of detection up to 100% gas streams. A palladium catalyst enables semi-quantification of the mixed isotopologues HD, HT and DT. The glovebox generates its own calibration standards, using calibrated vessels and PVTc calculations.

Note

This is the continuous-flow problem being attacked directly Klein et al. showed that a continuously operating fuel cycle cannot be stopped for physical inventory, and that flow-measurement uncertainty grows as √t. HERA's "real-time" framing targets exactly that, and its instrument choice is the best pair in the accountancy table — Raman at < 0.5% and mass spectrometry at 0.5–5% (Assessment of Challenges for Tritium Accountancy and Control in Fusion Energy Systems). Its self-calibration by PVTc is that table's "static PVTC" method used as the reference for the dynamic one.

THALIA — tritium trapped in liquid breeder materials. Develops and validates a method for accountancy of tritium held in liquid lithium, LiPb and FLiBe, with the first phase on FLiBe. Two sub-projects:

  • ERATO — sample preparation for analysing tritiated lithium-based salts by liquid scintillation counting (LSC)
  • CLIO — hydrogen analysis by ICP-MS, a technique the entry notes "is widely used but has not been developed for small atoms such as hydrogen and its isotopologues"

Both techniques are at low technology readiness level, principally because getting the tritiated lithium matrix into a state suitable for analysis is difficult. Stated challenges: working with lithium-based materials, unknown tritium speciation, very low concentrations, and sample storage.

Warning

Breeder-inventory accountancy is at low TRL, and that has a named consequence Sindelar et al. note that no method exists for measuring tritium production in a blanket, and this is the programme trying to build one — starting from a low TRL with tritium speciation in the breeder unknown.

It lands directly on a design already in this vault: ST-E1 breeds in liquid lithium, which is THALIA's third target material rather than its first. So the plant whose breeding claim this vault could not evidence also sits in the material class whose accountancy methods do not yet exist.

Access limitations#

Programme entry only — a conference description, not an abstract, and not a paper. The page was captured to raw/articles/ and the project names verified present in the captured HTML. There is no publication behind it in this vault.

No quantitative figures of any kind. No detection limits, no tritium concentrations, no accuracies, no timelines and no funding figure appear on the page. Everything above is a statement of intent and approach.

Single-speaker programme entry: Clemence Mont, UKAEA, a 30-minute talk in Session 3-11. The institutional authority is UKAEA's, not a peer-reviewed result's — hence confidence: medium.

What this source does not establish#

  • No results. Nothing has been measured, validated or published. "Low technology readiness level" is the entry's own characterisation.
  • No evidence that either technique will work. ICP-MS for hydrogen isotopes is explicitly undeveloped, and LSC sample preparation for tritiated lithium salts is the stated obstacle rather than a solved step.
  • Nothing about liquid lithium or LiPb specifically, which are named as targets beyond the FLiBe first phase. The breeder most relevant to ST-E1 is the least advanced here.
  • No link to a regulatory requirement. The programme is developing capability; nothing states what standard it would satisfy, and per Assessment of Challenges for Tritium Accountancy and Control in Fusion Energy Systems no IAEA or NRC requirement for tritium exists to satisfy.
