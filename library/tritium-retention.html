Tritium that enters plant structures and stops — trapped in the first wall, divertors and breeding blanket rather than circulating through the fuel cycle. It is the term every fuel-cycle model in this vault omitted until Meschini et al. 2025.

Why omitting it was not conservative#

Trapping does not merely park inventory; it slows the whole cycle, because trapped tritium is unavailable to breed the next plant's start-up charge. For an ARC-class plant, accounting for trapping, irradiation-induced traps and component replacement raises first-wall and vacuum-vessel inventory by a factor of 10³–10⁴, and carries through as:

Quantity Change
Doubling time +50%
Start-up inventory +30%
Required TBR +2–5%

Two effects compound it. Neutron damage creates new traps as the plant runs (dpa accumulating in tungsten and structure), and scheduled component replacement physically removes trapped inventory from the plant.

Warning

It enters accountancy as a residual, not a measurement Tritium Accountancy records that no instrument exists to measure tritium production in the blanket or consumption in the plasma. Retention is therefore inferred from what the balance fails to close — so the term with the largest modelled uncertainty is the one measured least directly. Sindelar 2023 states such methods "would need to be developed".

What is not known#

The 10³–10⁴ figure is ARC-class with a FLiBe immersion blanket. No source in this vault models retention for a liquid-lithium spherical tokamak, and ST-E1's fuel-cycle paper assigns its first wall and divertor only 0.012–0.024 kg — three orders below the ARC-class trapped figure, on a different model with no trapping physics. The two are not comparable, and this vault does not transfer one onto the other.

No measurement exists for any of it: no D-T fusion power plant has operated, so every retention figure here is modelled.
