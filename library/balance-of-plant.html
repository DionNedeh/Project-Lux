Everything between the blanket and the grid: the primary heat transfer system (PHTS), the intermediate heat exchangers, the thermodynamic cycle, the turbine-generator, and the electrical systems that both feed the plant and export from it.

It is the majority of the plant and a minority of the discussion. Balance of plant is 30–40% of capital cost (Document 30), capping how much core innovation can reduce total cost.

The chain that sets efficiency#

Conversion efficiency is 35–45% for a Rankine or closed Brayton cycle, and is not a free parameter: it follows from coolant outlet temperature, which follows from the structural material's temperature limit, which follows from irradiation behaviour — the whole path from neutron damage to electricity price running through one materials link. What the plant consumes to run itself is then subtracted as recirculating power.

Warning

Say which efficiency Cycle efficiency can be quoted against gross generated power or net of coolant pumping, and the two differ by around two percentage points on the same design. Helios reports ~40.2% net of pumping and ~42.2% on total generated power, explicitly noting the second convention is used "in some fusion power plant design studies". An unqualified cycle efficiency is as ambiguous as an unqualified Q.

A worked design case#

Helios is the vault's only end-to-end company-authored accounting (Overview of the Helios Design: A Practical Planar Coil Stellarator Fusion Power Plant, a design study, not a measurement). Lead-lithium and helium carry heat to intermediate heat exchangers; steam superheated to 635 °C drives three turbines. Of 1,094 MW thermal, 459.9 MWe is generated and 69.8 MW of that runs the plant, leaving 390.1 MWe net — itemised in Recirculating Power.

Two architectures, and why they differ#

Set that against EU-DEMO, whose pre-concept balance of plant is the public-programme counterpart (Maturation of Critical Technologies for the DEMO Balance of Plant Systems):

Helios EU-DEMO
Operation steady-state 2 h pulse, ~10 min dwell
Energy storage none molten salt, required
Primary coolant systems — 8 (WCLL) to 14 (HCPB)

A fission EPR has one primary circuit at 4,300 MWth. DEMO needs eight to fourteen at roughly half that — the sharpest available statement of why fusion balance of plant is not borrowed technology. The loops themselves are primary heat transfer systems; the buffering problem is thermal storage, which a Stellarator avoids entirely.
