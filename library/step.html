The Spherical Tokamak for Energy Production — the UK's flagship, and a prototype fusion power plant rather than an experiment. Targets net electricity around 2040, delivered by UK Industrial Fusion Solutions Ltd.

Sited at West Burton, Nottinghamshire, a former coal-fired power station.

2026 progress#

  • First land parcel transferred from EDF on 23 March 2026 — three and a half years ahead of schedule
  • £200 million Construction Partner contract to the ILIOS consortium (Kier–NUVIA JV)
  • Tokamak Energy selected as Magnet Systems Partner (£70m / $95m)
  • Site capacity tripled to ~120 professionals
  • First public consultation ran 14 January – 11 March 2026
  • August 2026: Dennis Whyte appointed CEO of UK Atomic Energy Authority — the MIT PSFC director and SPARC/ARC co-architect now leads the agency delivering a spherical tokamak

Its distinguishing bet is that the spherical configuration's high beta compensates for lower achievable field. Its distinguishing risk is the centre column, which must carry conductor and shielding in almost no space — a risk ST-E1's neutronics quantifies for a sibling design at ≈ 50 dpa per full-power year and a magnet life well short of intent.

Fuel cycle, modelled externally#

Meschini et al. 2023 model two STEP-class variants — one with a liquid-lithium blanket, one with an encapsulated breeding blanket of FLiBe pebbles, gas-cooled — and find required TBR below 1.2 achievable only with burn efficiency of 0.5–1%, availability above 70%, processing under 4 h and DIR. An independent academic model of a STEP-class machine, not UKAEA's own fuel-cycle analysis, which this vault does not hold.

UKAEA's vanadium strategy names the spherical tokamak with a liquid-metal breeder blanket as its target asset, with V-4Cr-4Ti qualification as the enabling work.
