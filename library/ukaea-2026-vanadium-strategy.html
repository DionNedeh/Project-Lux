The first source in this vault to address the vanadium alloy supply chain — a dependency Critical Materials and Supply Chains had never analysed, despite ST-E1 selecting vanadium over RAFM steel.

Warning

A strategy poster, not a peer-reviewed study This is a conference presentation by UKAEA's manufacturing group, stating an organisation's intent and summarising others' published work. source_type: notes. Its claims about alloy behaviour are second-hand summaries of the five papers it cites; its claims about strategy are UKAEA's own. Nothing here is a measurement made for this document.

The claim that evidences something this vault held as asserted#

The high thermal conductivity and favourable nuclear properties of V-4Cr-4Ti alloys "also reduce the need for neutron multipliers or lithium-6 enrichment in liquid lithium breeder blanket systems."

That is a third party — a national laboratory with no stake in ST-E1 — stating the architectural logic that ST-E1's unenriched-lithium choice depends on. The vault previously recorded that logic as asserted and unevidenced. It is now evidenced in outline, though still not quantified: no TBR figure accompanies the statement. See Lithium-6.

Manufacturing scale, which is the real constraint#

Heat Scale Route
Industrial heat (Johnson & Smith) 1200 kg Aluminothermic-reduction V + high-purity Cr/Ti, EBM purification, double vacuum-arc melting
NIFS-HEAT-1 (Muroga et al.) 30 kg (200 kg planned) EBM of V, Cr, Ti; vacuum arc remelting
SWIP-30 (Fu et al.) 30 kg Double electron-beam melting in high vacuum

The largest heat ever made is 1200 kg. Set against any power-plant-scale requirement, this is the gap the poster exists to address: UKAEA names "Investing in Domestic UK Vanadium material & manufacturing supply chain" as a long-term bet, and a "White Paper on Industrialisation of V-4Cr-4Ti" as an intended output. The supply chain is presented as something to be built, not something that exists.

Liquid lithium: a second-order problem named plainly#

  • A standard composition with impurity limits does not yet exist and needs specifying.
  • "Purity of commercial Li today is lower than that in 80s/90s" — commercial lithium has become worse, not better, for this application.
  • Lithium strips oxygen from vanadium, preventing embrittlement; but carbon and nitrogen in the lithium diffuse into the alloy.
  • MHD pressure drop requires insulator coatings, which are also wanted as tritium permeation barriers and for corrosion resistance.

Open work, in UKAEA's own words#

Radiation-accelerated corrosion and cracking, transmutation-produced helium effects on similar and dissimilar joints, and high-temperature oxygen effects at higher dose all "need to be studied". Qualification is being attempted by a novel digital route — Bayesian optimisation over literature and tensile data to 900 °C, producing a surrogate model, with EPRI engaged for regulatory acceptance — funded by a £250k DSIT tactical fund, with ORNL, Tokamak Energy, the University of Birmingham, ATI and digiLab named as partners. The target asset is explicitly a spherical tokamak with a liquid-metal breeder blanket.

What it does not establish#

  • No tonnage requirement, and no production-capacity figure. Neither global vanadium output nor fusion demand appears anywhere in this document.
  • No TBR number for the enrichment claim above.
  • Its reference [1] is not held: Muroga, Zheng & Yang, Current Opinion in Solid State and Materials Science 36 (2025) 101224 — see Vanadium Alloys for that access failure.
