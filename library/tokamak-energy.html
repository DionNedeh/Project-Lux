companies

Oxfordshire. 300 staff. Spherical tokamak developer and a merchant magnet business through its TE Magnetics division.

  • ST40 achieved 100 million K in 2022 — the first privately funded device to do so
  • Demo4 reached 11.8 T and 7 MA-turns — the first full-scale tokamak-configuration HTS magnet demonstration (2026)
  • Selected as Magnet Systems Partner for the UK's STEP programme — £70 m / $95 m

Successor device: ST80-HTS, which in turn informs the design of the ST-E1 power plant — a 5 m, aspect-ratio-2.3, 5.25 T pre-concept design built around phased operation: 300–500 MWe net in a pilot phase, 800–1000 MWe after a planned upgrade, with permanent magnet cage and vessel separated from replaceable in-vessel systems (Tokamak Energy's Pre-concept Design for a Fusion Power Plant: An Overview of ST-E1, an 11-paper Focus Collection). A US Department of Energy — Office of Fusion Milestone Program awardee.

Notable for breeding on pure, unenriched lithium — a deliberate route around the ⁶Li enrichment chokepoint (Lithium-6) — with V-4Cr-4Ti structure in the centre-column shield in place of RAFM steel. Both choices were evidenced on 2026-11-02 when the Focus Collection's neutronics and fuel-cycle papers became reachable; the company also builds its own neutronics and fuel-cycle codes, G4Tokamak and PyFuel (Simulation, Codes and Nuclear Data).

Note

The company publishes against its own criteria, including where it falls short ST-E1's neutronics paper reports peak fast flux to the HTS magnets at about 2.4× its own stated limit, giving ~13 full-power years against a 30 fpy intent (Nuclear Technology Considerations and Neutronics for the ST-E1 Fusion Power Plant, Superconductors and Magnet Engineering). That is a shortfall disclosed in a company-authored paper — worth recording as a point about this company's disclosure practice, not only about the design.

The magnet business matters beyond its revenue: alongside Commonwealth Fusion Systems selling to Realta Fusion, it is evidence of a fusion supply chain forming rather than 56 vertically integrated prototypes. See Fusion Beyond Electricity.
