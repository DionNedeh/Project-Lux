Routing most of the plasma exhaust straight back to the fuelling system, bypassing the isotope separation system altogether. An EU-DEMO concept, and the cheapest available reduction in a plant's tritium inventory.

The logic#

Few per cent of fuelled tritium burns (Tritium Burn Fraction). The rest returns to the exhaust essentially as it went in — a hydrogen-isotope mixture whose composition is close to what the fuelling system wants anyway.

Full isotope separation by cryogenic distillation is slow, and the isotope separation system dominates the inner cycle's processing time. Every hour spent in a distillation column is tritium sitting in holdup: capital locked up at $30,000–35,000/gram, decaying at 5.5% per year, and appearing in the plant's safety case as potential release.

DIR's claim is that most of that exhaust never needed separating.

The quantified effect#

From Abdou et al. 2021, which credits the EU-DEMO concept explicitly: at η_f · f_b = 1%, recycling 80% of exhaust directly lowers the required TBR from 1.120 to 1.075.

That is a 0.045 reduction in required TBR bought with plumbing rather than neutronics — and it moves the requirement from the middle of Abdou's possible band toward his high-confidence band (≤1.05). Against a working margin of only 10%, that is a significant purchase.

The mechanism is simple: shorter processing means less tritium in holdup, so a smaller start-up and reserve inventory, so less surplus the blanket must breed.

Status#

A concept with roadmap backing, not a demonstrated system. DIR is a DOE roadmap milestone, and the frontier in tritium processing is inventory reduction rather than throughput (Document 28 §6).

Two cautions belong on the record:

  • No integrated fuel cycle has ever run, with or without DIR. The 80%/1.075 figure is model output from a system-level dynamics code.
  • Two further designs make DIR a requirement, not an option. ST-E1 specifies a DIR fraction of 0.7, with protium at 1 at% in / 0.8 at% out, and Meschini et al. 2023 name DIR as one of three conditions for self-sufficiency in ARC- and STEP-class plants.
  • The sensitivity is not uniform. Helios's own study found DIR fraction among the parameters that barely move system dynamics, against availability, doubling time and burn fraction which dominate (Overview of the Helios Design: A Practical Planar Coil Stellarator Fusion Power Plant). Not flatly contradictory — different designs at different operating points — but the vault records both, and DIR's benefit should not be assumed design-independent.
