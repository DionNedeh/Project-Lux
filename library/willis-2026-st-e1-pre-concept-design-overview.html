The authoritative design document for ST-E1, Tokamak Energy's pre-concept fusion power plant, and the overview heading an 11-paper Focus Collection. Received 19 December 2025, revised 12 February 2026, accepted 20 March 2026, published 10 July 2026. 85 authors, predominantly Tokamak Energy with contributions from PPPL, ORNL, INL and Columbia.

This capture closes the gap recorded at the previous ingest, where ST-E1's parameters reached the vault only at second hand through a fuel-cycle preprint.

The design is a financing argument#

The stated problem is not physical but commercial: pilot plants are needed to de-risk fusion, yet their limited lifetime energy output and high upfront capital cost make them hard to fund. ST-E1's answer is phased operation with a designed upgrade path, separating permanent assets from replaceable ones so that operating data can be bought without writing off the capital-intensive parts.

Permanent (full plant life) Replaceable
Low-aspect-ratio magnet cage — TF, PF and CS coils Breeding blanket system
Vacuum vessel, neutron shielding, cryostat In-vessel structural items
Civil structures and plant Divertor and other plasma-facing components
High-performance neutron shielding
Elements of H&CD and diagnostics
In-vessel auxiliary control and field-correction coils

Replaceable segments are engineered for 5 full-power years, with tungsten armour tiles potentially needing more frequent replacement in the high-damage mid-plane region.

Warning

The paper writes Q bare Table 1 gives "Q (–)" with no subscript, and the prose says only "fusion gain". The vault does not accept an unqualified Q (Q — the Four Gain Definitions). Arithmetic on the paper's own rows identifies it: 1500 MW / 75 MW = 20 exactly for the pilot phase, and 2000–2500 / 25–50 spans the quoted 50–70 for the commercial phase. It is therefore Q_sci — fusion power over auxiliary heating power — as derived by this vault, not as labelled by the source. Recorded as a derivation, not a quotation.

The finding with the widest consequences: unenriched lithium#

ST-E1's breeder is pure, unenriched lithium, with vanadium-alloy structure and helium cooling in the initial configuration. The paper's stated rationale is to achieve a high volumetric TBR "without reliance on Li-6 enrichment" or extensive use of toxic multipliers such as beryllium, while keeping radioactive waste acceptable.

This matters because the vault has recorded ⁶Li enrichment to 30–90% as a requirement of breeding blankets and as a sovereignty chokepoint concentrated in Russia and China (Lithium-6, Critical Materials and Supply Chains). ST-E1 is a design route that sidesteps that chokepoint entirely — plausibly because a low-aspect-ratio spherical tokamak affords thick, high-coverage blanket volume. It does not invalidate the enrichment finding for other designs; it establishes that enrichment is a property of particular blanket architectures rather than of D-T fusion.

Vanadium alloys are chosen over RAFM steel for a wider allowable temperature window and favourable neutron absorption, enabling higher temperatures and improved thermodynamic efficiency. This explains the V-4Cr-4Ti pipework modelled in Multiscale Assessment of Tritium Behavior in Preliminary Fusion Pilot Plant Design Using Surrogate Models in TMAP8. Shielding is tungsten carbide in modular units, plus boron carbide, RAFM and stainless steels.

Other figures worth carrying#

  • Commercial phase is steady-state, requiring fully non-inductive current sustainment with high self-driven bootstrap fraction. Pilot phase retains a solenoid (30 Vs flux swing).
  • Pedestal and density: κ = 1.3–1.6, Greenwald density fraction at the pedestal top f_GW = 0.8–1.0, q_min ≳ 2.2 to avoid sawteeth and low-order neoclassical tearing modes, core radiated fraction 45–69%.
  • An upgrade path is deliberately preserved for the blanket: greater use of liquid lithium as a self- or dual-cooled option in the commercial configuration could reduce recirculating power by up to 200 MW and raise thermal cycle efficiency. Stated as an upgrade option, not a design.
  • Grid behaviour: a 5-minute current ramp to full fusion power while capping peak grid draw at 500 MW needs only 1–2 MWh of on-site storage — notably modest against the thermal storage burden carried by pulsed designs, because ST-E1 is not pulsed.

Internal inconsistency preserved#

The paper gives two TBR figures. Table 1's RDP states TBR > 1.14; §3.2 states the "target of the TBR is ~1.1, leaving a sufficient margin to cover decay and processing losses". These are not the same requirement, and the paper does not reconcile them. Both are recorded; see Tritium Breeding Ratio (TBR) and Known conflicts in Research library.

Access limitations#

Inspected in full. The 11-page article was captured to raw/ and extracted to raw/extracts/; every figure above was read from that extract. CC BY 4.0, confirmed both in the article text and by OpenAlex (oa_status: gold, licence cc-by).

Note

The route to this capture, and why it is recorded The publisher's own PDF link is CAPTCHA-gated. IOP's PDF URL returned a Radware Bot Manager Captcha HTML page rather than the article. That download was discarded without being finalised into raw/ — a 14 KB HTML file named .pdf is not evidence. No attempt was made to work around the CAPTCHA.

OpenAlex lists only that CAPTCHA-gated IOP URL as a PDF location for this CC BY 4.0 article; the DOAJ record carries a landing page and no full text. The copy captured here came from OSTI (osti.gov/servlets/purl/3378206, OSTI ID 3378206), a legitimate DOE repository route reached because DOE-funded co-authors deposited it under contracts AC02-09CH11466 and SC0024889.

The captured file is the version of record, not an accepted manuscript: it carries IOP's "PAPER · OPEN ACCESS" banner, the typeset Nucl. Fusion 66 (2026) 086001 (11pp) header, the DOI and an IOP download stamp. OSTI's own metadata flags it journal_type: AM (accepted manuscript), which the file itself contradicts. The file is trusted over the metadata, and the disagreement is recorded rather than resolved silently.

This is the second CC BY 4.0 fusion paper in this vault whose publisher route is CAPTCHA-blocked, after Tang et al. on Helios balance of plant. The pattern is worth noting: open licence does not imply open access in practice.

Both are now held as Crossref metadata with complete abstracts only — 086003 and 086004 — because their full texts are unreachable by every route tried. What this source does not establish#

  • Nothing is built, and nothing is measured. This is a pre-concept design. Every figure is a target, a requirement or a systems-code output. ST-E1 has no hardware; Tokamak Energy's hardware evidence remains ST40 and the Demo4 magnet.
  • The TBR is not demonstrated and the unenriched-lithium claim is not validated here. The volumetric-TBR argument is asserted in this overview and referred to the companion neutronics and tritium papers (086003, 086004), neither of which is held. No breeding blanket has operated in any device.
  • The availability target is a target. >80% sits far above anything fusion has demonstrated, and the paper itself identifies availability as a dominant economic determinant whose maintenance cost and schedule drivers are still being identified.
  • CapEx is an estimate against an unbuilt plant. $12–14k/kW is a design goal; the paper notes that even if ST-E1 misses its normalised CapEx or availability targets it would still provide significant value — which is an explicit acknowledgement that it may miss them.
