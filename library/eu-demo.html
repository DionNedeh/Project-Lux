Europe's demonstration power plant — the most conservative major design, deliberately.

The European strategy has been to let ITER retire the physics risk and then build a machine that requires no new physics. DEMO is consequently large (major radius around 9 m), operates in conventional ELMy H-mode, uses a pulsed inductive scenario with thermal storage, and targets roughly 2 GW fusion for ~500 MWe net.

It must do three things ITER does not: breed its own tritium, deliver net electricity to the grid, and achieve utility-grade Availability and RAMI.

EU-DEMO is also the origin of direct internal recycling, credited with cutting processing time and inventory and with lowering required TBR from 1.120 to 1.075 at η_f · f_b = 1% (Physics and Technology Considerations for the Deuterium–Tritium Fuel Cycle and Conditions for Tritium Fuel Self Sufficiency) — the corpus's clearest case of a plumbing decision buying breeding margin.

Two blanket concepts have been carried in parallel: HCPB (helium-cooled pebble bed, beryllium multiplier) and WCLL (water-cooled lithium-lead).

The balance of plant it implies#

Carrying two blankets means carrying two balance-of-plant designs, and the pre-concept phase settled a reference for each: WCLL takes direct coupling with a small molten-salt store, HCPB takes indirect coupling with a store large enough that the turbine never notices the dwell (Maturation of Critical Technologies for the DEMO Balance of Plant Systems).

The plant-level consequence is scale. DEMO needs 8 separated primary coolant systems under WCLL and 14 under HCPB, with 10 and 14 primary heat exchangers or steam generators respectively — against one primary circuit and four steam generators in a European Pressurized Reactor running at 4,300 MWth to DEMO's ~2,300 (Primary Heat Transfer System (PHTS)). Conservatism on the physics side has not bought simplicity on the plant side.

The critique#

Conservatism makes it large; large makes it expensive. The design has been re-baselined repeatedly and construction start has slipped with ITER's schedule. EUROfusion's roadmap targets DEMO construction in the 2030s and fusion electricity on the European grid by 2050.

Waste inventory. DEMO could generate as much as 10,000 t of solid waste from in-vessel components alone — roughly four times the ILW, HLW and spent-fuel total of a European sodium-cooled fast reactor — per Overview on the Management of Radioactive Waste from Fusion Facilities. The figure is a conceptual-design upper bound, not a measured inventory. See Fusion Waste: Classification, Clearance and Disposal.
