An instrument paper from the Tritium Laboratory Karlsruhe (IAP-TLK) at KIT — one of very few facilities licensed to handle gram-scale tritium — describing a cryogenic spectroscopy cell in scientific commissioning.

Note

This is an enabling-data source, not an accountancy source T2ApIR does not measure plant inventories and makes no accountancy claim. It belongs in this cluster because the measurement it serves sits underneath accountancy: laser Raman spectrometry is the most accurate tritium concentration method in the accountancy table at < 0.5% (Assessment of Challenges for Tritium Accountancy and Control in Fusion Energy Systems), and spectroscopic concentration measurement is only as good as the reference data behind it. The vault records the connection as that, and not as a claim the authors make.

The stated gap#

"The literature on experimentally verified material properties of tritium is sparse" — and the paper names four places that scarcity bites: pellet production for magnetic confinement (Pellet Injection), target fuelling for inertial confinement, cryogenic distillation (the isotope-separation workhorse of the tritium plant), and neutrino and rare-event physics.

That is a notable finding in itself: the properties of the fuel are less well characterised than the machines built to burn it.

What the instrument does#

  • Investigates all six hydrogen isotopologues (H₂, HD, D₂, HT, DT, T₂) and their mixtures in gaseous, liquid and solid phases, plus the dynamics of phase changes
  • Mixtures with noble gases — xenon and neon — can also be studied
  • A cryogenic measurement cell reaching below 10 K, with optical access for infrared absorption spectroscopy, Raman spectroscopy and a polariscope, alongside temperature and pressure measurement
  • Ortho-para states are among the declared subjects

What this source does not establish#

  • No results that this vault can use yet. The experiment is in scientific commissioning — "first results" here means the apparatus works, not that material-property data has been published. The vault takes no tritium property value from it.
  • No accountancy performance figure. No detection limit, sensitivity or concentration accuracy appears, so it cannot be compared against the < 0.5% Raman figure it ultimately supports.
  • Nothing about a fusion plant. This is a laboratory cell handling laboratory quantities, not an in-line instrument on a fuel cycle.
