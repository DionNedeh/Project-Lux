The fraction of tritium fuelled into the plasma that actually fuses before confinement is lost. Formally, burning rate over fuelling rate (Abdou et al. 2021).

It is small, and that is the problem. Nearly every atom injected returns unburnt for re-processing — so burn fraction, not breeding, sets a plant's tritium inventory.

Why it dominates the fuel cycle#

Unburnt tritium still has to be handled, and time in the exhaust train, isotope separation and storage is inventory — bought before startup, decaying while it waits. So what matters is never f_b alone but its product with fuelling efficiency.

η_f · f_b Consequence (3 GW plant)
0.36% — ITER's estimate at maximum fuelling Required TBR 1.82, start-up inventory >200 kg: unacceptable
2% Start-up inventory 11 kg at 4 h processing
5% Required TBR 1.02, start-up inventory <5 kg at 1 h processing

Requirement: η_f · f_b > 2%, ideally 5%. Below 1% it fails however good the blanket is.

Warning

Never write burn fraction bare f_b and η_f · f_b differ by about fourfold at reference fuelling efficiency. The ST-E1 modelling paper tabulates a "tritium burn efficiency" of 0.02 and never defines it — the same class of defect as unqualified Q.

The route to raising it#

Fuelling efficiency η_f has a reference value of 25%, with DEMO extrapolations below it. The favoured route is high-field-side pellet injection, projected at 25–50% — projections, not measurements, and ones Abdou et al. call "highly uncertain" at shallow penetration. Detail on Pellet Injection.

The deeper obstacle: it cannot be measured where it matters. DEMO predictions are "highly uncertain because of lack of experiments with burning plasma". Helium Ash dilution caps it further.

Note

Corroborated independently — and now with figures, 2026-11-02 ST-E1's fuel-cycle paper reports extraction sensitivity and fuelling efficiency as the dominant drivers, rather than marginal improvements in breeding ratio — Abdou's reframing, by a different method with no shared authorship. Its full text gives f_b = 2.6% at η_f 76% (R = 0.1) or 2.9% at 68% (R = 0.2), for a burn efficiency of 2%; it does not say which is the reference.

That 2% clears Meschini et al. 2023's 0.5–1% requirement — but Meschini attach availability > 70%, processing < 4 h and DIR, and ST-E1's figure assumes 100% availability (Availability and RAMI). The margin is real; the operating point is not demonstrated.

Status#

Never demonstrated at reactor-relevant values. No device has operated a burning D-T plasma long enough to establish f_b in a reactor regime, and the figures above are a mixture of ITER projections and design requirements. See Engineering Challenges and Open Problems and Risks.
