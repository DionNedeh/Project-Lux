Compress fuel so violently that it burns before it can fly apart. Confinement is provided by nothing but the fuel's own inertia; the confinement time is the disassembly time, of order nanoseconds.

The sequence, in about 20 nanoseconds#

  1. Drive — 192 beams at National Ignition Facility deliver ~2 MJ at 351 nm, peaking near 500 terawatts
  2. Ablation — the capsule surface blows off, driving the shell inward as a rocket at 350–400 km/s under ~100 megabars
  3. Stagnation — hot spot at ~50 μm radius and ~10 keV; surrounding cold fuel at several hundred g/cm³, roughly 1,000× solid density
  4. Burn — ~100 picoseconds

Convergence ratio 25–40. That is the crux: compressing a shell 30× in radius while keeping it spherical to a few percent means suppressing Rayleigh-Taylor Instability in ICF throughout.

Density and time are inverted relative to magnetic confinement — ~10³¹ m⁻³ for ~10⁻¹⁰ s, against ~10²⁰ m⁻³ for seconds. Eleven orders of magnitude apart, with the same Lawson product to satisfy, which is why a laser fusion plant and a tokamak share the fuel and essentially nothing else.
