Reduced-Activation Ferritic-Martensitic steels — EUROFER-97 (EU), F82H (Japan), CLAM (China). The baseline structural material for a fusion power plant.

The design principle#

Build alloys from elements whose activation products are short-lived: tungsten for molybdenum, tantalum for niobium, and eliminate nickel, cobalt and copper where possible.

Done well, the structure decays to hands-on or recyclable levels in 50–100 years rather than fission's geological timescales. Reduced-activation design is a waste-management requirement, not an optimisation.

The operating window, and why it is narrow#

~350–550 °C, both ends set by irradiation: below ~350 °C, hardening and embrittlement dominate; above ~550 °C, thermal creep and Helium Embrittlement take over. Every degree is worth money, because cycle efficiency scales with coolant outlet temperature.

Alternatives, all less mature#

ODS steels (to ~650 °C, nano-oxide dispersions that trap helium), vanadium (~400–700 °C, oxygen-sensitive), SiC/SiC (>1000 °C, immature).

Note

The industrial gap DOE's near-term milestone is to establish domestic industrial heat capability for multi-tonne Fe-alloy and 500 kg V-alloy heats. The US cannot yet melt fusion-grade structural steel at scale.

Two designs that decline RAFM, and what it costs them#

Helios keeps EUROFER-97 for the blanket but takes V-4Cr-4Ti ("V44") for the first wall, citing higher operating temperature, no ferromagnetism and damage tolerance for a 15 full-power-year first-wall life against "less than half that" for RAFM (Overview of the Helios Design: A Practical Planar Coil Stellarator Fusion Power Plant). ST-E1 takes V-4Cr-4Ti for the centre-column shield box and as baseline for the centre-column helium manifold — but does not abandon RAFM: its neutronics specifies F82H for the lower overhead horizontal shield at 350–550 °C, and 316L(N)-IG for the vacuum vessel (Nuclear Technology Considerations and Neutronics for the ST-E1 Fusion Power Plant, read in full 2026-11-02). The earlier record here — vanadium "outright" — was the overview read alone.

Both pay for it. V44 has a high affinity for hydrogenic species, swells under irradiation, and has an immature supply chain: the largest heat ever produced is 1200 kg (UKAEA Vanadium Strategy (LIBRTI conference presentation)), against the DOE 500 kg milestone above. Nor does vanadium escape activation: ST-E1's own neutronics reports non-reduced-activation steels and PFCs as its highest-activating components. Design-study selections, not qualified material.

Waste classification. RAFM steels are the dominant fusion waste stream by mass, and their disposal class is set less by designed composition than by uncontrolled impurities — niobium, cobalt, potassium — arriving with commercial feedstock. An ORNL formulation meeting LLW requirements shows composition control is an effective lever (Overview on the Management of Radioactive Waste from Fusion Facilities, Fusion Waste: Classification, Clearance and Disposal).
