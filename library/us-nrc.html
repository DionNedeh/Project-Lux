On 26 February 2026 the NRC published a proposed rule, Regulatory Framework for Fusion Machines, regulating commercial fusion under the byproduct material framework (10 CFR Part 30) rather than the reactor licensing framework (Part 50) used for fission.

Comments closed 27 May 2026; a final rule is targeted for October 2026, well ahead of the 31 December 2027 deadline set by NEIMA.

Why it matters enormously#

  1. It regulates the material, not the machine — tritium and activation products, proportionate to the actual hazard
  2. Performance-based, technology-inclusive, risk-informed — necessary when 56 companies pursue a dozen different confinement schemes
  3. It devolves licensing to the states. As of February 2026 there are 39 Agreement States. Most future US fusion facilities will be licensed by a state radiation control programme, not the NRC — faster, cheaper, more predictable
  4. It removes a category of financing risk. Regulatory uncertainty is a discount-rate problem

The physical basis is sound: no fissile material, no chain reaction, no decay-heat meltdown pathway, and under a gram of fuel in the reacting volume. See Policy, Regulation and Safety.
