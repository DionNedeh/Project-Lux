historymachines

The Tokamak Fusion Test Reactor at Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory. The first magnetic device to run extensive experiments with a 50/50 deuterium-tritium mix — the fuel a real plant would use.

  • 10.7 MW fusion power (November 1994)
  • 1,500 MJ total fusion energy across 1993–97
  • Designated a Nuclear Historic Landmark, 2020

The record nobody has beaten#

Important

TFTR shot 83546, 17 February 1995: Triple Product = 7.9 × 10²⁰ keV·m⁻³·s. The highest triple product ever achieved in a tokamak — and it has stood for thirty-one years.

JET — Joint European Torus's 2021 and 2023 D-T campaigns reached 2.2 × 10²⁰ and 1.4 × 10²⁰ respectively, below the 1990s peaks.

This is not regression. The 1990s peaks were transient hero shots on carbon walls at 36–44 keV; modern campaigns optimise for duration, ITER-relevant wall materials and integrated scenarios. But the third reason is the important one: no machine capable of exceeding TFTR was built anywhere in the world between its shutdown in 1997 and the 2020s.

Magnetic fusion's long performance plateau was a procurement stall, not a physics stall — the cleanest quantitative evidence available against the "thirty years away" diagnosis. See The Discourse.
