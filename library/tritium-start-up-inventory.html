The tritium a D-T plant must possess on day one, before its blanket has bred anything — an entry fee paid in its scarcest material.

The numbers#

From Abdou et al. 2021:

Condition Start-up inventory
η_f · f_b = 2%, processing time 4 h, 3 GW 11 kg
η_f · f_b = 5%, processing time 1 h, 3 GW <5 kg
ITER's burn fraction (0.36%), 3 GW >200 kg — stated as unacceptable
100 MW facility ~1 kg

Inventory scales linearly with fusion power — plants want to be large, and largeness makes the entry fee unaffordable. Against a world civilian inventory of 30–40 kg (Tritium Breeding Ratio (TBR)) at $30,000–35,000/gram, an 11 kg charge is a third of all that exists; >200 kg exceeds world supply severalfold.

What drives it down#

  1. Burn fraction and fuelling efficiency — dominant; 2% to 5% more than halves the requirement.
  2. Processing time — 1–4 h, and the isotope separation system dominates it, which is why Direct Internal Recycling (DIR) reduces inventory directly.
  3. Reserve time — a 24 h reserve gives unacceptable inventories, so reserves must cover only a few hours, converting storage into a reliability problem.

Note

Two design claims, neither with a comparable figure Helios gives 1–2 kg against its own analysis concluding under 1 kg (Overview of the Helios Design: A Practical Planar Coil Stellarator Fusion Power Plant) — design value versus requirement, both far below Abdou's 3 GW figures and consistent with linear scaling from ~390 MWe. ST-E1 gives 1.58 kg including a 20% commissioning contingency, 1.31 kg tritium-lean, with a doubling time of 152 days. CORRECTION 2026-11-02: this page previously recorded ST-E1 as claiming "low overall inventory" with no figure attached — true of the abstract, not of the full text.

Warning

Trapping would raise every figure here by about a third Meschini et al. 2025 find start-up inventory +30% once trapping is modelled, for an ARC-class plant. Nothing on this page includes it.

Warning

Not the same as in-process inventory A smaller quantity circulates at any moment: ITER's is expected at ~1 kg, commercial plants at several hundred grams to 1 kg (Assessment of Challenges for Tritium Accountancy and Control in Fusion Energy Systems). That is the holdup the cycle minimises, not the charge a plant must own before starting. Same units, different quantities — see Tritium Accountancy.

Who supplies the first charge#

Nobody. Tritium comes mostly as a CANDU byproduct at under 0.13 kg per reactor-year and decays at 5.5% per year, so no stockpile can be built up in advance. Abdou et al. name the institutional answer — a fusion nuclear science facility sized not for power but to accumulate surplus tritium for DEMO's start-up, a brief no funded facility has. So this is a programme question, not a plant one.
