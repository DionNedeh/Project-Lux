The liquid breeder that does two jobs at once: a molten lead–lithium eutectic carrying Lithium-6 for tritium breeding while the lead acts as the neutron multiplier. It is the reason a large family of blanket designs exists.

Why it dominates blanket design#

Lead is abundant, cheap and comparatively non-toxic — the direct contrast with Beryllium, the alternative multiplier. Putting the multiplier and the breeder in the same flowing liquid removes a whole class of solid-component problems: no pebble beds to crack, no swelling, and a breeder that can be processed outside the vessel.

Three of the main blanket concepts in Power Plant Designs Compared are PbLi variants:

  • WCLL — water-cooled lithium-lead, an EU DEMO baseline candidate
  • HCLL — helium-cooled lithium-lead
  • DCLL — dual-coolant lithium-lead

The competing family is helium-cooled pebble bed (HCPB), which uses solid ceramic breeder with a Beryllium multiplier.

What it costs#

Polonium-210. Neutron capture on the bismuth impurity in lead breeds ²¹⁰Po, an intensely radiotoxic alpha emitter, into the circulating loop. The IAEA review underpinning Fusion Waste: Classification, Clearance and Disposal tracks ²⁰⁹Bi and ²¹⁰Po inventories in the 600 m³ of flowing PbLi in an ARIES-class plant, alongside ²⁰³Hg. This is a coolant that becomes a mobile radiological hazard in its own right, quite separate from the activated structure around it.

MHD pressure drop. Driving an electrically conducting liquid metal across a strong magnetic field induces currents that oppose the flow. The pumping penalty feeds directly into Recirculating Power, and it is the principal engineering objection to liquid-metal blankets.

Tritium extraction. Tritium must be recovered from the liquid at low concentration and high throughput — part of the fuel-cycle problem in Vacuum, Wall Conditioning and the Tritium Plant.

A specified instance#

Helios gives the vault its first fully parameterised PbLi blanket from a company design (Overview of the Helios Design: A Practical Planar Coil Stellarator Fusion Power Plant, a preconceptual design — nothing built). It is a uniform 50 cm layer completely surrounding plasma and first wall, 65% enriched in Lithium-6, with EUROFER-97 structure and helium cooling, and the liquid flowing at 6.6 cm/s before passing through a heat exchanger and the tritium extraction system. Silicon carbide inserts form the non-conductive break between flowing metal and structure — the named mitigation for the MHD pressure drop above. Idealised TBR is 1.3.

Note

Which eutectic This page uses Pb-16Li; the Helios paper writes Pb-17Li. The composition is quoted variously as Pb-15.7Li through Pb-17Li — nomenclature, not disagreement.
