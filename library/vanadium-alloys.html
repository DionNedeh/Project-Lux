V-4Cr-4Ti — "V44" — is the reference fusion structural alloy for liquid-metal breeder blankets, and the main alternative to RAFM steel. Per UKAEA's vanadium strategy it offers high-temperature creep resistance, high thermal strength, strong resistance to irradiation swelling, and low activation.

Why a blanket designer wants it#

High thermal conductivity and favourable nuclear properties "reduce the need for neutron multipliers or lithium-6 enrichment in liquid lithium breeder blanket systems" (UKAEA). That is the architectural argument behind ST-E1's choice of pure, unenriched lithium — and it routes around the Li-6 enrichment chokepoint recorded in Critical Materials and Supply Chains.

ST-E1's neutronics paper selects V-4Cr-4Ti as the structural material for the centre-column shield box, and as the baseline for the centre-column helium manifold with 316L(N)-IG as an alternative.

The constraint is production, not physics#

Warning

The largest heat ever made is 1200 kg Documented V-4Cr-4Ti production runs to a 1200 kg industrial heat, with NIFS-HEAT-1 at 30 kg (200 kg planned) and SWIP-30 at 30 kg (UKAEA, citing Johnson & Smith, Muroga et al. and Fu et al.). There is no standing commercial production line, and UKAEA names building a domestic supply chain and industrialising the alloy as long-term bets.

Chemistry bounds it too: impurity control is the limiting factor, and commercial lithium purity is reported as lower today than in the 1980s–90s.

Note

Escaping one chokepoint is not escaping activation ST-E1's own neutronics team reports that non-reduced-activation ferritic martensitic steels and plasma-facing components show the highest activation in the machine. A vanadium centre column does not make the plant low-activation overall. See Fusion Waste: Classification, Clearance and Disposal.

Demand figures, and whose they are#

The trade association Vanitec (post of 3 June 2026, author Dr Yu Li) puts a commercial fusion reactor at 500–1,500 t of V-4Cr-4Ti — roughly 460–1,380 t contained vanadium — against global production of "approximately 120,000 tonnes per year", and scales that to 46,000–138,000 t for a 100-reactor fleet. These are Vanitec's illustrative scenarios. The review they accompany — Muroga, Zheng & Yang, Current Opinion in Solid State and Materials Science 36 (2025) 101224 — is not held: ScienceDirect returned HTTP 403 on 2 November 2026, and no OSTI deposit was found despite an ORNL co-author. Whether the tonnages are the review's or Vanitec's arithmetic could not be determined.
