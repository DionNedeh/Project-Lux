icfmachines

192 laser beams, frequency-tripled to 351 nm, delivering ~2 MJ in a shaped nanosecond pulse at peak powers near 500 terawatts. Completed March 2009 at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory.

Its primary mission is stockpile stewardship — validating weapons physics without nuclear testing. Fusion energy is a secondary benefit of a weapons-physics facility, which explains its design priorities (single-shot, diagnostically rich, efficiency-indifferent). See Military, Dual-Use and Security.

Warning

Target gain is not net energy NIF's laser draws roughly 300–400 MJ from the grid to deliver 2.08 MJ on target. On a wall-plug basis the record shot's gain was about 0.02. See Q — the Four Gain Definitions.

The enabling change for the 2025 record was a higher-quality high-density-carbon (diamond) capsule — not the laser. See Rayleigh-Taylor Instability in ICF.

As public infrastructure. NIF represents about $3.5 bn of federal investment and is named, alongside PPPL and DIII-D, as facility capacity that private fusion companies now depend on but would not have built — the economics of a public good. See State Capital and the Public Balance Sheet.
