The quiet weak link under every neutronics calculation in fusion.

FENDL — the Fusion Evaluated Nuclear Data Library, coordinated by the International Atomic Energy Agency Nuclear Data Section — is the community standard for cross-section data feeding tritium breeding, shielding, activation, nuclear heating and damage calculations.

The state of it#

  • In FENDL-3.1d, neutron cross sections for 65 of 180 isotopes are inherited from ENDF/B-VII.1 — a general-purpose fission-oriented library
  • Neutron fluxes computed with different libraries differ by as much as +12% / −8%
  • Five isotopes were found to have negative average heating numbers — a physically impossible value, i.e. an outright data defect
  • Uncertainty propagation to gamma heating and shutdown dose rate has never been published by any study

Why it matters more than it sounds#

The Tritium Breeding Ratio (TBR) design target is 1.1 and the failure threshold is 1.0 — a 10% margin. Nuclear data uncertainty on TBR is a few percent, and inter-library flux discrepancies run to 12%.

The uncertainty in the calculation is a meaningful fraction of the entire design margin on the parameter that determines whether a fusion plant can operate at all.

The corollary: a blanket design validated only computationally has not been validated. That is precisely why UNITY-3 and LIBRTI matter more than their budgets suggest.

Transport codes: MCNP (export-controlled), OpenMC (open source, increasingly standard — and which had to fall back to ENDF/B-VIII.0 because of defects in converted FENDL-3.1d photon data).

A second design that does not use FENDL#

ST-E1's neutronics runs its in-house Geant4 application G4Tokamak on ENDF/B-VIII.0 with ParticleHP for transport, and TENDL-2019 under FISPACT-II for activation — not FENDL (Nuclear Technology Considerations and Neutronics for the ST-E1 Fusion Power Plant, full text read 2026-11-02, answering a question this page previously left open).

That makes two of the vault's recent neutronics lineages routing around FENDL-3.1d. It also gives the first cross-code metric held here: against MCNP on a 20° sector, G4Tokamak's mean flux ratio is 0.90 — a ~10% underestimate — while returning a 2% higher TBR. No tolerance or statistical test is stated, so this is a reported comparison, not a validation. See Simulation, Codes and Nuclear Data.
