The most important fusion device ever built. The only machine to have run three separate D-T campaigns, and holder of the record for total fusion energy from a magnetic device.

R = 2.96 m, a = 1.25 m, B₀ = 3.45 T, I_p = 4.8 MA. First plasma 25 June 1983, ahead of schedule and under budget. Ceased plasma operations December 2023 after forty years.

The D-T record#

Campaign Result Q_sci
1991 PTE 1.7 MW — first controlled fusion power in a magnetic device ~0.15
1997 DTE1 16.1 MW peak, 22 MJ 0.27 harmonised (0.67 transient peak)
2021 DTE2 59 MJ, ITER-like Be/W wall 0.33
3 Oct 2023 DTE3 69 MJ over ~5 s from 0.2 mg of fuel 0.37

See Q — the Four Gain Definitions and Appendix: Quantitative Progress Data on why 0.67 and 0.27 are both correct.

The more valuable legacy#

DTE3's real value was not the record. It demonstrated quasi-continuous exhaust and the X-point radiator — advanced Edge-Localised Mode (ELM)-avoiding exhaust regimes — in actual D-T plasmas. Directly what ITER needs.

JET is also the only tokamak ever maintained fully remotely. No human has entered its vessel in over thirty years. Its MASCOT system replaced the entire first wall in 18 months of two-shift working.

96 research papers were submitted within a year of the final campaign. The Decommissioning and Repurposing programme runs to ~2040 — the world's first dismantling of a tritium-contaminated tokamak.

First real waste inventory. Every waste tonnage in Fusion Waste: Classification, Clearance and Disposal is a conceptual-design estimate; no fusion power plant has been decommissioned. JET's decommissioning and repurposing programme is the largest such exercise underway, and is where those estimates will first meet measured reality.
