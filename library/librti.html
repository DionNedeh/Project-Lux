Lithium Breeding Tritium Innovation — a £220 million UK Atomic Energy Authority facility, described as a global first-of-a-kind for tritium fuel technology.

Total investment £220 m across 2025/26 – 2029/30, within the UK's £2.5 bn five-year fusion commitment.

Together with UNITY-3, it is where Tritium Breeding Ratio (TBR) moves from calculation to measurement.

The accountancy instruments#

Added 2026-10-19. LIBRTI is not only breeding a ratio — with UKAEA's Tritium Fuel Cycle division it is building the measurements that tritium accountancy currently lacks (Development of Analytical Techniques for Tritium Accountancy Around the Fusion Fuel Cycle (HERA and THALIA)). Two named projects:

  • HERA — real-time tritium analysis in gas streams, by mass spectrometry and Raman spectrometry, quantifying H₂, D₂ and T₂ from the limit of detection to 100%, with a palladium catalyst for semi-quantification of HD, HT and DT, and a glovebox that generates its own calibration standards by PVTc.
  • THALIA — accountancy for tritium trapped in liquid breeders: FLiBe first, then liquid lithium and LiPb. Sub-projects ERATO (liquid scintillation counting of tritiated lithium salts) and CLIO (ICP-MS for hydrogen isotopes, a technique not previously developed for atoms this small).

Both are at low technology readiness level, with tritium speciation in the breeder unknown.

That matters for the assessment below: measuring tritium in a breeder is a harder and less mature problem than breeding it, and no method currently exists for quantifying blanket tritium production at all. LIBRTI is addressing a gap that the vault's other sources only name.

Added 2026-11-02. LIBRTI's conference programme is also where UKAEA set out its V-4Cr-4Ti strategy (UKAEA Vanadium Strategy (LIBRTI conference presentation)), naming a V44/liquid-lithium self-cooled blanket as its long-term bet. That places the structural alloy and the breeder in the same programme — appropriate, since lithium strips oxygen from vanadium while carbon and nitrogen in the lithium diffuse into it, so neither can be qualified independently of the other.

Strategically, this is one of the best-chosen public investments in fusion anywhere. The UK did not attempt to compete on machine size; it invested in the two things nobody else was adequately funding — lithium breeding and, through H3AT, tritium handling.
