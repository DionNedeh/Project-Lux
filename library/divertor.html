The component that handles plasma exhaust — and the binding physics constraint on fusion power density.

The numbers#

A 500 MW fusion plasma must exhaust roughly 100 MW, channelled onto a strike region only millimetres wide.

Value
Material limit, steady-state 5–20 MW/m²
Tungsten design target < 10 MW/m²
Unmitigated reactor heat flux ~10× above the limit
Required target plasma temperature single-digit eV (below the sputtering threshold)

The problem worsens with machine quality. The scrape-off layer width narrows as machines get larger and fields stronger (Eich scaling, λ_q roughly inversely proportional to poloidal field) — so compact high-field designs face it most acutely. See The B⁴ Scaling.

The only known answer is Detachment. Alternative geometries — Super-X Divertor, X-point radiator, Negative Triangularity — and liquid-metal surfaces are the active research fronts.

Every design study for thirty years has identified heat exhaust, not confinement, as the limit on power density. See Power Plant Designs Compared.

The stellarator's separate divertor problem#

Stellarators cannot simply inherit the tokamak poloidal divertor, and their own options have not clearly scaled. The experimentally proven frontrunner is the island divertor used on W7-X — but its proponents accept it does not scale directly to a power plant, because neutral compression at the target is insufficient for a practical vacuum pumping scheme.

Helios claims a route around this: a toroidally continuous, non-resonant X-point divertor, tokamak-like, which its authors state is the first time a stellarator equilibrium has been explicitly designed to contain one, and the first pairing of such a divertor with a power-plant-relevant equilibrium at finite beta and bootstrap current (Overview of the Helios Design: A Practical Planar Coil Stellarator Fusion Power Plant). The higher neutral compression is what lets the design use turbomolecular rather than cryosorption pumps — better at pumping helium and needing no regeneration, at the cost of long ducts to keep the pumps magnetically shielded.

Assumed target heat flux is 10 MW/m², at the tungsten design target above rather than below it. This is a design study: the divertor exists in simulation only.
