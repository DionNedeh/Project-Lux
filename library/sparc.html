The most important machine in fusion right now. A compact, high-field, D-T Tokamak whose sole purpose is to be the first magnetically confined plasma to produce net fusion energy.

Parameter Value
Major radius R / minor radius a 1.85 m / 0.57 m
Toroidal field on axis 12.2 T
Plasma current 8.7 MA
Superconductor REBCO High-Temperature Superconductor HTS
Design goal Q_sci > 2
Nominal projection Q ≈ 11, ~140 MW (at H98,y2 = 1)
Projected Triple Product 6.2 × 10²¹ — ~8× the best ever achieved

Built by Commonwealth Fusion Systems with MIT PSFC at Devens, Massachusetts. Its design philosophy is the direct application of The B⁴ Scaling: rather than build something ITER-sized, build something JET-sized with more than twice the field.

Status, August 2026#

  • ~80% assembled as of 30 July 2026; TF1, first of 18 magnets, installed
  • All 18 magnets due in place by end of summer 2026
  • First plasma and scientific breakeven targeted 2027 (slipped from 2026)

Why it matters beyond CFS#

DOE's roadmap names "Milestone 1: Q > 1, first demonstration in a U.S. fusion device" — which is SPARC. A clean result validates the entire high-field HTS thesis and compresses every compact-tokamak schedule behind it. A visible failure would re-rate the whole sector, including companies whose physics has nothing to do with tokamaks — see The Investment Mechanics.

Its physics basis was published openly as a seven-paper Journal of Plasma Physics special issue in 2020.
