The physics that only a Burning Plasma can test.

3.5 MeV alphas are super-Alfvénic — faster than the Alfvén speed — and in a burning plasma they carry a substantial fraction of the plasma's energy with a strong spatial gradient. Such a population can resonantly drive Alfvén eigenmodes: TAEs (toroidicity-induced), RSAEs (reversed-shear), EPMs (energetic particle modes).

The self-defeating loop#

Alphas drive the mode → the mode transports the alphas outward → the alphas no longer heat the plasma → and on the way out they can strike the wall in localised spots.

Whether this is benign or severe at reactor scale is not settled. It cannot be resolved by scaling from present machines, because present machines cannot make a comparable alpha population. It is one of the specific things ITER exists to determine.

In three dimensions#

For stellarators this is a design constraint rather than a phenomenon. Fast alphas in a 3D field follow complex orbits and an unoptimised configuration loses them promptly. Good fast-alpha confinement is a separate and harder objective than thermal transport — see Quasi-symmetry and Quasi-isodynamicity — and a live question for every stellarator power plant design.

Alpha channelling — using waves to extract energy from alphas and give it to fuel ions before they thermalise — would improve heating efficiency and remove Helium Ash faster simultaneously. It appears in the Aneutronic Fusion (p-¹¹B) literature as one of few routes that could make p-¹¹B economic. Not demonstrated.
