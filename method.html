Our purpose

Project Lux explores how AI can contribute to more effective energy systems, beginning with fusion. We connect experimental results to the engineering questions around them. The public resource is a research library and a place to learn.

Lux does not claim an operating fusion plant, a validated plant controller, or measured energy savings. Its companion project Spira examines AI’s electricity and water demands. Valens is another Voelori resource, with its own subject and evidence library.

Read the result at its actual scale

Experimental control

A method tested on a physical research device for a specific task, duration, and operating envelope.

Offline evaluation

A representation, prediction, or forecast tested on data. This alone is not live machine control.

Simulation or design

A result depends on modeled assumptions and inputs. A proposed plant’s performance is not a measured operating result.

Reported or announced

A claim attributed to an organisation, survey, or announcement. Keep the claim’s source and date attached.

Peer review and demonstration type are different questions. A peer-reviewed simulation remains a simulation; a source note is not an independent replication.

Library confidence and status come from the original note’s editorial metadata. “High confidence” or “stable” does not certify that every statement is correct or current. Source limitations, conflicts, and dates take priority.

How the citations connect

Homepage citations [L01]–[L08] open short briefs with bounded findings and links to the full library notes. Each note includes its revision date, original Markdown, related research, and incoming links. Study briefs also link to the primary paper.

The bibliography collects the wider source trail. A failed retrieval is an access limitation, not evidence that a study or result does not exist. Metadata-only captures are labelled in the source notes, including L03 and L04.

A dated library, made readable

This release publishes 282 research notes from the existing Lux wiki. It adds navigation, resolved internal links, accessible table containers, and text search. The original Markdown accompanies each note; the public build does not replace research with screenshots or images of text.

Notes are written and maintained with AI assistance and human curation. They include synthesis and interpretation as well as reported findings. Publication is not a comprehensive new fact-check of the corpus. Research dates remain separate from the date the site was published.

Downloaded third-party papers, internal research logs, and personal workspace files are not distributed by this site. The source notes identify captures held locally and link to primary publishers or open archives where available. No new redistribution licence is asserted over third-party material.

The complete site is static. Search runs in your browser; there are no accounts, submitted search queries, tracking analytics, or contact forms. Visiting an external source follows that provider’s own practices.

Keep the energy accounting clear

Fusion output, plasma heating, total electrical input, and net electricity exported describe different system boundaries. Improving an experiment’s prediction or control metric does not itself prove a lower whole-system energy cost. Read the energy-balance guide and the gain definitions note.

Diagrams on the homepage are conceptual illustrations. They are not plasma simulations, facility measurements, or control interfaces.

Corrections and continuing work

Use the Project Lux issue tracker to report an error, with the page URL, exact claim, and a primary source where possible. Responses are not guaranteed on a particular schedule. Corrections should preserve the source trail and be recorded in project updates.
